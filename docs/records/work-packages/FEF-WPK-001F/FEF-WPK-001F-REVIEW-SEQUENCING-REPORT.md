# FEF-WPK-001F — Review Sequencing Report

**Programme:** Founder Engineering Framework  
**Work package:** FEF-WPK-001F  
**Report date:** 2026-07-24  
**Status:** Strategic Sequencing Recommendation — Not an Operational Plan  
**Review status:** Not Commenced  
**Execution effect:** None

## 1. Purpose

Explain and test the strategic domain sequence recommended by [FEF-FGRA-001](../../../governance/reviews/FEF-FGRA-001-FOUNDER-GOVERNANCE-REVIEW-AGENDA.md).

This report does not create the Review Plan, identify sessions, allocate dates, assign participants, prepare evidence packs, or instantiate review outputs.

## 2. Recommended Sequence

```text
D1 — Governance Authority
        ↓
D2 — Evidence Governance
        ↓
D3 — Governance Assurance
        ↓
D4 — Records and Information Governance
        ↓
D5 — Governance Lifecycle and Evolution
        ↓
D6 — Framework Administration
        ↓
D7 — Constitutional and Standards Boundary
        ↓
D8 — Open Question Final Disposition
```

D8 question mapping is cross-cutting throughout. Its position at the end represents final synthesis and disposition, not the first time questions may be considered.

## 3. Dependency Analysis

### 3.1 D1 Before All Other Domains

Evidence weight, validation authority, record ownership, lifecycle transitions, identifier governance, constitutional placement, and question closure all depend on knowing who holds the relevant authority.

Starting elsewhere risks producing technically coherent recommendations that have no agreed decision owner.

### 3.2 D2 Before Substantive Assurance

The review requires admissible, attributable, and preserved evidence before it can define assurance outcomes. Otherwise, validation may test documents without knowing whether their sources can support governance conclusions.

### 3.3 D3 Before Records and Lifecycle

Assurance establishes which reviews, approvals, validations, risk acceptances, and exceptions need records. It also distinguishes lifecycle readiness from substantive approval.

### 3.4 D4 Before Lifecycle

Lifecycle states and transitions require durable records of versions, decisions, approvals, applicability, and supersession. Establishing lifecycle before record obligations would make states difficult to evidence.

### 3.5 D5 Before Administration

Identifiers, registers, and taxonomies should support known lifecycle and change needs. Administration designed first tends to encode accidental structures.

### 3.6 D6 Before Constitutional Boundary Synthesis

Framework Administration helps expose which controls are operational mechanisms rather than enduring constitutional principles. This makes later extraction more disciplined.

### 3.7 D7 After Validated Domain Outcomes

The Constitution/standards boundary cannot be evaluated safely from abstract preferences. It needs concrete, validated authority, evidence, assurance, records, lifecycle, and administration outcomes.

### 3.8 D8 as Cross-Cutting Mapping and Final Synthesis

Existing Open Questions should inform domain examination, but starting with question closure could anchor the review to a draft standard’s structure. Final dispositions should follow the broader governance analysis.

## 4. Alternative Sequencing Approaches

### Alternative A — Evidence Governance First

```text
Evidence → Authority → Remaining Domains
```

**Strength**

Signals immediate commitment to provenance after historical evidence loss.

**Weakness**

Evidence authority, admission decisions, confidentiality, and mandatory controls themselves depend on who may establish them.

**Assessment**

Not recommended as the primary order. Evidence preparation may begin operationally in parallel, but substantive evidence-governance decisions should follow the authority examination.

### Alternative B — Open Questions First

```text
Existing Open Questions → Domain Review
```

**Strength**

Provides a visible checklist and may appear efficient.

**Weakness**

Anchors the new baseline to questions derived from FEF-RGS-000 rather than examining governance architecture independently. It may miss more fundamental issues or encourage premature closure.

**Assessment**

Not recommended. Use the Open Questions as mapped inputs across domains, then complete their dispositions during synthesis.

### Alternative C — Constitutional Boundary First

```text
Constitution Boundary → Detailed Governance Domains
```

**Strength**

Establishes an early target for later document architecture.

**Weakness**

Encourages premature constitutional abstraction before evidence, findings, and Founder Decisions exist.

**Assessment**

Rejected. It conflicts with the Charter’s separate-extraction principle.

### Alternative D — Lifecycle Before Records

```text
Authority → Evidence → Assurance → Lifecycle → Records
```

**Strength**

Defines states before deciding what to store.

**Weakness**

Lifecycle states become theoretical if the review has not established the records that prove transitions and applicability.

**Assessment**

Not preferred. Records should precede lifecycle, with controlled iteration if lifecycle analysis exposes new record needs.

### Alternative E — Parallel Domain Review

```text
Authority and Evidence
      ↙       ↓       ↘
Assurance   Records   Lifecycle
```

**Strength**

May reduce elapsed time and reveal cross-domain relationships early.

**Weakness**

Increases coordination, contradictory assumptions, repeated Founder decisions, and traceability complexity.

**Assessment**

Potentially useful only after D1 and D2 are sufficiently stable and only if the future Review Plan defines integration gates. The Agenda does not authorise parallel execution.

### Alternative F — Engineering-Need-Led Review

```text
Immediate Engineering Needs → Governance Decisions
```

**Strength**

May produce fast answers for near-term implementation.

**Weakness**

Allows implementation pressure to define constitutional governance, expands scope, and undermines governance-before-engineering.

**Assessment**

Rejected for the constitutional baseline review.

### Alternative G — Risk-First Review

```text
Highest Perceived Risk → Remaining Domains
```

**Strength**

Targets urgent exposures.

**Weakness**

Risk acceptance and ownership depend on authority, evidence, and assurance concepts that have not yet been established.

**Assessment**

Risk should influence prioritisation within each domain, not replace the dependency sequence.

## 5. Iteration and Feedback

The recommended sequence is directional rather than strictly one-way.

The future Review Plan may permit:

- a later domain to return a clarification need to an earlier domain;
- evidence discovered later to trigger revalidation;
- a deferred question to move to a later domain;
- constitutional-boundary analysis to identify an unresolved authority issue;
- final Open Question synthesis to expose an incomplete domain outcome.

Iteration must preserve:

- canonical record history;
- source and version traceability;
- prior dispositions;
- explicit reasons for reopening;
- Founder authority boundaries.

## 6. Decision-Fatigue Control

The sequence reduces decision fatigue by:

- resolving authority concepts before asking detailed authority-dependent questions;
- grouping related evidence and decisions by domain;
- separating analytical findings from reserved decisions;
- deferring administrative detail until fundamental governance is known;
- presenting constitutional candidates only after validated domain outcomes;
- consolidating Open Question disposition at the end.

The future Review Plan should avoid presenting Founder decisions merely to complete a numerical quota.

## 7. Sequencing Risks

| Risk | Sequencing Control |
|---|---|
| Early domains become too abstract | Tie examination to admitted evidence and current governance needs |
| Later critical domain receives insufficient attention | Preserve D7 Critical priority despite late position |
| D8 becomes an end-loaded backlog | Map questions continuously and validate mappings per domain |
| Iteration becomes uncontrolled | Require recorded reopening rationale and integration gates |
| Session count is mistaken for schedule | Keep Matrix ranges indicative until Review Plan approval |
| Founder decisions are repeated across domains | Maintain cross-domain decision traceability |

## 8. Conclusion

The recommended sequence is appropriate because it moves from authority and epistemic foundations, through assurance and durable operation, to constitutional synthesis and question disposition.

It provides the future Review Plan with a dependency-aware structure without scheduling execution or predetermining outputs.
