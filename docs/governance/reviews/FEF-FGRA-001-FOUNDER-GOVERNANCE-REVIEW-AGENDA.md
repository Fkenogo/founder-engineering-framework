**Execution effect:** None — this Agenda does not schedule or commence the review

## 1. Purpose

The Founder Governance Review exists to establish a fresh, attributable governance baseline for the Founder Engineering Framework after the evidence supporting the historical review was determined to be unrecoverable.

This Agenda defines:

- what the future review should examine;
- why each subject matters;
- the relative priority of each governance domain;
- the recommended strategic order of examination;
- the classes of governance output expected;
- the strategic conditions for review readiness and completion.

This Agenda guides the later preparation of an operational Review Plan. It is not the Review Plan and does not assign a review identifier, sessions, dates, participants, questions, issues, findings, decisions, or constitutional candidates.

## 2. Relationship to the Charter

FEF-FGRC-001 defines how the future review must operate. This Agenda defines what the review should examine and in what strategic order.

The Agenda:

- is subordinate to the Charter;
- cannot reduce a Charter evidence, authority, traceability, validation, or approval control;
- cannot authorise a session or review output;
- becomes a required strategic input to the future Review Plan only after Founder approval;
- must be returned for amendment if it conflicts with the approved Charter.

If the Charter changes before approval, this Agenda must be checked and, where necessary, revised before the Review Plan is prepared.

## 3. Strategic Objectives

The future review should pursue the following governance outcomes without predetermining their substantive answers.

### SO-1 — Establish Attributable Governance Authority

Clarify which governance authority exists, which authority is reserved to the Founder, what may be delegated, and how transitional authority operates before a complete constitutional baseline exists.

### SO-2 — Establish an Evidence Basis for Governance

Determine how governance claims, findings, recommendations, and decisions will be supported, challenged, traced, and preserved.

### SO-3 — Establish Governance Assurance

Clarify the distinct roles of validation, technical review, Founder approval, activation, risk acceptance, exception handling, and final assurance.

### SO-4 — Establish Records and Information Integrity

Determine what governance records must exist, who maintains them, how their integrity and access are protected, and how organisational knowledge is retained.

### SO-5 — Establish Controlled Governance Evolution

Clarify lifecycle states, versioning, applicability, supersession, withdrawal, transitional treatment, and controlled change.

### SO-6 — Establish Proportionate Framework Administration

Determine the minimum identifiers, classifications, taxonomies, registers, and administrative controls needed for consistent operation.

### SO-7 — Establish the Constitutional Boundary

Distinguish enduring framework principles from domain governance, standards, procedures, templates, and implementation detail.

### SO-8 — Produce Explicit Open Question Dispositions

Ensure that each existing and newly admitted governance question has a traceable status and that no question is treated as closed without attributable Founder authority.

### SO-9 — Produce a Validated Review Baseline

Produce a review record whose evidence, findings, decisions, deferrals, uncertainty, and downstream implications can be independently traced and validated.

## 4. Review Philosophy

### 4.1 Evidence Before Opinion

The review should distinguish:

- admissible evidence;
- contextual information;
- analytical interpretation;
- recommendation;
- Governance Finding;
- Founder Decision.

No conclusion should receive greater authority merely because it is repeated, confidently stated, or operationally convenient.

### 4.2 Founder Authority

The Founder retains the decision authority reserved by the Charter. Advisers, reviewers, validators, and coding agents may structure evidence and options but may not infer or exercise Founder authority.

### 4.3 Governance Before Engineering

The review should establish the governance basis required to direct later framework and engineering activity. Existing implementation preferences must not determine governance conclusions.

### 4.4 Constitutional Extraction After Validated Decisions

Constitutional candidates should arise only from admissible evidence, validated findings, and attributable Founder Decisions. Candidate identification is not constitutional drafting or approval.

### 4.5 Transparency

The review should preserve uncertainty, disagreement, rejected alternatives, evidence gaps, conditions, deferrals, and non-decisions.

### 4.6 Traceability

Every substantive output should be traceable through the Charter chain from evidence to Review Question, session, GF, FD where needed, Open Question disposition, and later authorised work.

### 4.7 Simplicity

The review should prefer clear, reusable governance concepts over excessive categories, roles, states, registers, and approval layers.

### 4.8 Minimum Necessary Governance

Governance should address demonstrated authority, evidence, assurance, information, lifecycle, and risk needs. It should not regulate matters that can remain within bounded professional judgment.

### 4.9 Separation of Strategic and Operational Design

This Agenda identifies strategic subjects and order. The future Review Plan shall determine operational sessions, evidence packs, roles, and validation activities.

### 4.10 Preservation of Historical Integrity

The new review must remain separate from the unrecoverable historical review. It may examine current questions afresh but may not recreate or inherit missing historical GF or FD content.

## 5. Prioritisation Method

Domain priority reflects the consequence of failing to obtain a sound governance outcome, not merely the order in which a domain appears.

| Priority | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Critical | The review baseline would be unsafe or structurally incomplete without a validated outcome from this domain |
| High | The domain materially affects governance reliability, scalability, or downstream work and requires explicit treatment |
| Medium | The domain is required for coherent operation but should be shaped by higher-priority authority and lifecycle outcomes |
| Low | The domain may be deferred without undermining the minimum viable governance baseline |

No Charter domain is classified Low in Draft v0.1. Each domain is Charter-required. Framework Administration is Medium because its detailed shape should follow, rather than drive, the more fundamental authority, evidence, assurance, information, and lifecycle outcomes.

Priority does not override dependency order. D7 is Critical but should be examined late because constitutional boundary conclusions depend on the preceding governance domains.

## 6. Domain Agenda

### 6.1 D1 — Governance Authority

**Priority:** Critical

**Why it matters**

All later governance depends on knowing who may decide, approve, delegate, validate, accept risk, grant exceptions, and amend governing instruments.

**Strategic examination**

- Founder reserved authority;
- constitutional authority;
- delegated governance;
- accountability and decision ownership;
- transitional authority before constitutional consolidation;
- non-delegable decisions;
- boundaries between programme, framework, domain, and project authority.

**Expected output classes**

- Review Questions concerning authority;
- Governance Findings on authority gaps, conflicts, or required distinctions;
- Founder Decisions where authority must be established or reserved;
- Deferred Matters where later constitutional or legal input is required;
- potential Constitutional Candidates.

**Dependencies**

- approved Charter;
- current authority statements and non-authority notices;
- FAR-001 and FAR-002 within their recorded scope;
- attributable Founder direction for the new review.

**Downstream dependency created**

D2–D8 require a stable understanding of authority to determine evidential weight, approval rights, lifecycle control, and final dispositions.

### 6.2 D2 — Evidence Governance

**Priority:** Critical

**Why it matters**

The prior evidence loss demonstrates that governance without preserved provenance cannot support durable authority. The new baseline must be reproducible and attributable.

**Strategic examination**

- evidence classes and hierarchy;
- admissibility and rejection;
- provenance and integrity;
- conflicting evidence;
- transparency and explainability;
- uncertainty and absence;
- contextual versus authoritative material;
- retention of evidence chains.

**Expected output classes**

- Review Questions about evidence treatment;
- Governance Findings on gaps, conflicts, and necessary controls;
- Founder Decisions on mandatory governance evidence requirements;
- Deferred Matters involving confidentiality, law, security, or unavailable sources;
- Open Question Dispositions related to provenance and evidence hierarchy.

**Dependencies**

- D1 authority context;
- Charter evidence rules;
- historical evidence audit and recovery lessons as context, not reconstructed governance.

**Downstream dependency created**

D3–D8 require agreed evidence treatment before their findings and decisions can be relied upon.

### 6.3 D3 — Governance Assurance

**Priority:** High

**Why it matters**

Evidence alone does not determine when governance is sufficiently reviewed, validated, approved, or ready for use. Assurance controls prevent validation from being confused with authority.

**Strategic examination**

- validation and review distinctions;
- Founder approval and reserved dispositions;
- activation and effective treatment;
- risk acceptance;
- exceptions and deviations;
- assurance independence;
- treatment of conditional or inconclusive outcomes.

**Expected output classes**

- Review Questions on assurance gates;
- Governance Findings on role conflicts and missing controls;
- Founder Decisions on approval, risk, exception, or lifecycle authority;
- Constitutional Candidates concerning reserved approval or risk authority;
- Deferred Matters requiring later implementation design.

**Dependencies**

- D1 authority;
- D2 evidence governance.

**Downstream dependency created**

D4–D8 rely on clear assurance and approval concepts for records, states, administrative controls, and final review completion.

### 6.4 D4 — Records and Information Governance

**Priority:** High

**Why it matters**

Governance remains auditable only when records, decisions, evidence, versions, and organisational knowledge are preserved and accessible under explicit controls.

**Strategic examination**

- governed record types;
- registers and custodianship;
- retention and archival;
- access, confidentiality, privacy, and security;
- legal hold and deletion;
- institutional knowledge;
- information lifecycle and integrity checkpoints.

**Expected output classes**

- Review Questions about mandatory records and responsibilities;
- Governance Findings on information gaps and preservation risks;
- Founder Decisions on minimum record obligations or reserved access decisions;
- Deferred Matters involving legal, security, privacy, or technical implementation;
- Constitutional Candidates concerning transparency or preservation duties.

**Dependencies**

- D1 authority and accountability;
- D2 evidence;
- D3 assurance and validation records.

**Downstream dependency created**

D5 lifecycle and D6 administration require defined records and custodianship to operate reliably.

### 6.5 D5 — Governance Lifecycle and Evolution

**Priority:** High

**Why it matters**

Governance must change without losing history, authority, applicability, or clarity. Undefined states create false claims of approval, activation, or obsolescence.

**Strategic examination**

- drafting and review states;
- approval versus operational treatment;
- applicability and transition;
- versioning;
- amendment;
- supersession and withdrawal;
- exceptions and expiry;
- controlled evolution;
- treatment of legacy governance.

**Expected output classes**

- Review Questions about lifecycle states and transitions;
- Governance Findings on ambiguous status or change controls;
- Founder Decisions on reserved transitions and effective treatment;
- Deferred Matters concerning migration or implementation;
- Constitutional Candidates concerning controlled evolution.

**Dependencies**

- D1 authority;
- D3 assurance;
- D4 records and preservation.

**Downstream dependency created**

D6 administration and D7 constitutional separation require a consistent lifecycle vocabulary and change model.

### 6.6 D6 — Framework Administration

**Priority:** Medium

**Why it matters**

Identifiers, classifications, taxonomies, registers, and roadmap relationships make governance usable, but excessive administration can become a substitute for judgment.

**Strategic examination**

- identifier principles;
- taxonomy and classification boundaries;
- register requirements;
- roadmap and work-package relationships;
- document relationships;
- simplicity and tailoring;
- minimum viable administration.

**Expected output classes**

- Review Questions on administrative consistency;
- Governance Findings on duplication, ambiguity, or unnecessary complexity;
- Founder Decisions where a permanent identifier or classification rule is required;
- Deferred Matters for detailed operating design;
- Open Question Dispositions concerning identifiers and administrative ownership.

**Dependencies**

- D1 authority;
- D4 record types and custodianship;
- D5 lifecycle and versioning.

**Downstream dependency created**

D7 needs clear administrative and domain boundaries to distinguish constitutional principles from operating mechanisms.

### 6.7 D7 — Constitutional and Standards Boundary

**Priority:** Critical

**Why it matters**

FEF requires a durable distinction between enduring constitutional governance and domain-specific or implementation-specific controls. Without that boundary, the Constitution risks becoming operationally overloaded and standards risk redefining fundamental authority.

**Strategic examination**

- enduring governance principles;
- constitutional versus standard-level content;
- inheritance and delegation;
- framework-wide versus research-domain concepts;
- candidate abstraction;
- transitional governance;
- separate consolidation and amendment authority;
- non-effects on FEF-RGS-000.

**Expected output classes**

- Review Questions about constitutional placement;
- Governance Findings on enduring versus domain-specific content;
- Founder Decisions on constitutional direction or reserved boundaries;
- Constitutional Candidates;
- Deferred Matters requiring later consolidation;
- implications for FEF-WPK-001C and FEF-WPK-001D.

**Dependencies**

- substantive outcomes from D1–D6;
- validated evidence and findings;
- attributable Founder Decisions where authority is implicated.

**Downstream dependency created**

Constitutional consolidation cannot begin until D7 outcomes and the final review record are approved.

### 6.8 D8 — Open Question Resolution

**Priority:** High

**Why it matters**

The review must demonstrate how existing uncertainties were handled. A governance baseline that leaves question treatment implicit cannot support later consolidation or RGS amendment.

**Strategic examination**

- mapping of all 23 existing Open Questions;
- newly admitted questions;
- answered, deferred, superseded, or out-of-scope treatment;
- closure authority;
- unresolved dependencies;
- downstream ownership.

**Expected output classes**

- Review Question mappings;
- Open Question Dispositions;
- related GFs and FDs where issued;
- Deferred Matters;
- residual risk and downstream dependency statements.

**Dependencies**

- the existing Open Questions Register;
- outcomes from all preceding domains;
- explicit Founder Decisions for resolution or closure.

**Downstream dependency created**

The final review validation and constitutional consolidation require a complete question-disposition matrix.

**Cross-cutting treatment**

D8 mapping should occur throughout the review, but final disposition should follow the substantive domain sequence. This does not create Review Questions or close any Open Question in this Agenda.

## 8. Proposed Strategic Review Sequence

```text
Governance Authority
        ↓
Evidence Governance
        ↓
Governance Assurance
        ↓
Records and Information Governance
        ↓
Governance Lifecycle and Evolution
        ↓
Framework Administration
        ↓
Constitutional and Standards Boundary
        ↓
Open Question Final Disposition
```

### 8.1 Sequencing Logic

1. **Authority first:** the review must know who may establish every later control.
2. **Evidence second:** reliable governance needs an agreed basis for claims and decisions.
3. **Assurance third:** the review must distinguish validation from approval before designing records or states.
4. **Records fourth:** evidence and assurance outputs require durable information controls.
5. **Lifecycle fifth:** lifecycle states depend on authority, assurance, and preserved records.
6. **Administration sixth:** identifiers and registers should implement known governance needs rather than define them.
7. **Constitutional boundary seventh:** extraction requires validated outcomes from the preceding domains.
8. **Open Question final disposition eighth:** question status should be synthesised only after substantive examination, while mapping continues throughout.

The sequence is strategic, not a schedule. The future Review Plan may propose controlled overlap or iteration when it preserves dependencies and Charter traceability.

## 9. Expected Review Output Classes

No output listed in this section is created by this Agenda.

### 9.1 Review Questions

Future operational questions that define the exact matter to be examined, its decision purpose, scope, evidence, and expected treatment.

### 9.2 Governance Issues — Future Enhancement

Potential structured records of governance tensions, inconsistencies, gaps, or risks that require analysis before a GF can be drafted.

Governance Issues are not defined as a required Charter artefact. They may be introduced only through an approved Charter-compatible Review Plan or controlled Charter enhancement that defines their schema, authority boundary, lifecycle, and traceability. This Agenda creates none.

### 9.3 Governance Findings

Evidence-based conclusions produced and lifecycle-controlled under the Charter. A GF does not create Founder authority or constitutional effect.

### 9.4 Founder Decisions

Exact attributable determinations issued only by the Founder under the Charter’s reserved authority and lifecycle controls.

### 9.5 Constitutional Candidates

Traceable propositions that pass the Charter’s candidate tests and are reserved for later authorised consolidation. They are not constitutional principles.

### 9.6 Deferred Matters

Questions, conflicts, dependencies, or decisions intentionally postponed with an attributable rationale and future trigger where supplied.

### 9.7 Open Question Dispositions

Traceable statuses for existing and newly admitted questions. No disposition constitutes closure without the authority required by the Charter.

### 9.8 Validation Records

Evidence, session, GF, FD, question, candidate, and final-review validation records required by the Charter.

## 10. Strategic Completion Criteria

The Agenda can be considered strategically complete when:

1. every Charter domain has a stated rationale, priority, dependency, and expected output class;
2. the strategic objectives do not predetermine substantive governance answers;
3. domain priorities are explicit and justified;
4. the recommended sequence preserves authority, evidence, assurance, records, lifecycle, administration, and extraction dependencies;
5. Open Question mapping is recognised as cross-cutting and final disposition remains downstream;
6. expected outputs are defined as future classes rather than instantiated records;
7. risks and strategic controls are documented;
8. the relationship between Charter, Agenda, Review Plan, and review execution is explicit;
9. validation confirms that no operational planning or review activity has occurred;
10. the Founder dispositions the Agenda for use as a required Review Plan input.

Strategic Agenda completion is not review completion. It does not satisfy the Charter’s session, GF, FD, evidence, validation, or final Founder approval criteria.

## 11. Strategic Risks and Controls

| Risk | Strategic Effect | Agenda Control |
|---|---|---|
| Scope expansion | Review absorbs standards, implementation, or unrelated programme work | Charter domains, explicit exclusions, and Review Plan scope gate |
| Premature constitutional drafting | Candidates become constitutional text before decisions are validated | D7 late sequencing and separate consolidation |
| Engineering bias | Existing implementation preferences determine governance | Governance-before-engineering philosophy and engineering exclusion |
| Evidence gaps | Findings or decisions rely on unverified assertions | D2 Critical priority and Charter admissibility controls |
| Decision fatigue | Founder receives too many poorly structured decisions | Dependency-based sequencing, issue consolidation, and minimum necessary governance |
| Over-governance | Excess roles, states, registers, and controls reduce usability | Simplicity principle and D6 Medium priority |
| Authority ambiguity | Review participants make decisions outside their role | D1 first and Founder reserved authority |
| Record loss | New baseline repeats the historical evidence failure | D4 High priority and contemporaneous records |
| Sequencing rigidity | Necessary feedback between domains is prevented | Review Plan may propose controlled iteration |
| Open Question tunnel vision | Existing questions constrain discovery of more fundamental issues | Domain-led examination with question mapping, not question-led governance design |
| Governance Issue proliferation | Future issue records become an unmanaged backlog | Issues remain an optional controlled enhancement |
| Historical contamination | Missing GF/FD content is treated as inherited | Separate review namespace and historical non-substitution |
