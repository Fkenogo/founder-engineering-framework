## 1. Purpose

Protect review integrity when operational roles prepare, control, record, validate, or decide review matters.

## 2. Independence Principles

- preparation and validation should be separated wherever practicable;
- custody does not determine evidential meaning;
- analysis does not validate itself;
- recording does not create authority;
- Founder authority remains distinct even when the Founder performs operational work;
- a role combination never combines authority;
- conflicts and limitations must be visible before reliance;
- unavailable independence must result in a disclosed exception and compensating control, not silent self-approval.

## 3. Mandatory Separation Rules

| Activity | Required Separation |
|---|---|
| RQ preparation and validation | The sole drafter should not be the sole Validator |
| Evidence Pack preparation and freeze validation | Evidence Custodian prepares; Validator independently validates |
| GF preparation and GF validation | Review Analyst prepares; Validator validates |
| Session recording and session-record validation | Review Recorder prepares; Validator validates |
| FD issue and FD-record validation | Founder issues; Recorder records; Validator validates the record |
| Constitutional Candidate preparation and validation | Analyst prepares; Validator tests candidate criteria |
| Register entry and independent gate validation | Preparing custodian or administrator must not be the only validator |

The Founder’s substantive decision is not “validated” for correctness. Validation checks only the decision record’s exactness, authority source, conditions, boundaries, and traceability.

## 4. Conflict of Interest

A conflict exists where a role holder’s personal, professional, financial, project, authorship, reputational, or prior-decision interest could reasonably affect—or appear to affect—neutral performance.

Potential conflicts include:

- validating one’s own sole preparation;
- reviewing evidence whose source or outcome the role holder controls;
- suppressing contrary evidence to protect prior work;
- recording a decision in which the Recorder advocates a preferred outcome without disclosure;
- advising and validating the same technical conclusion;
- holding an external obligation inconsistent with evidence access or disclosure;
- using Founder access to bypass operational controls.

## 5. Mandatory Disclosure

Before acting on an affected artefact, the role holder must disclose:

- role and individual;
- affected artefact or activity;
- nature of the actual, potential, or perceived conflict;
- whether the conflict is temporary or continuing;
- proposed mitigation;
- residual limitation;
- escalation or approval authority;
- required independent revalidation;
- effective and end dates.

The disclosure must be linked from the Role Assignment Register and the affected validation or session record.

## 6. Multiple-Role Assignments

One individual may temporarily hold multiple roles when:

- the review is Founder-operated or resource-constrained;
- no practicable independent assignee is available;
- the combination is necessary to prevent administrative delay;
- the combination does not purport to delegate Founder authority;
- the combination, affected artefacts, and limitations are recorded;
- compensating controls are defined before reliance.

### 6.1 Normally Compatible Combinations

Subject to disclosure:

- Review Administrator and Review Analyst;
- Review Administrator and Review Recorder for preparatory records;
- Evidence Custodian and Review Recorder where evidence references are mechanically recorded.

### 6.2 High-Risk Combinations

These require explicit Founder approval and compensating controls:

- Validator with any preparation role for the same artefact;
- Evidence Custodian and Validator for the same Evidence Pack;
- Review Analyst and Validator for the same GF;
- Review Recorder and Validator for the same session or FD record.

### 6.3 Founder-Operated Review

The Founder may hold one or more operational roles while retaining Founder authority. Every act must identify the capacity used.

If the Founder is also the sole Validator:

- the independence limitation must be disclosed;
- validation must be limited to objective record and control checks;
- no validation may be represented as independent;
- a later independent revalidation must occur where practicable before downstream reliance;
- any residual procedural risk must be expressly accepted by the Founder in Founder capacity.

## 7. Compensating Controls

Depending on the conflict, controls may include:

- independent second-person review;
- delayed independent revalidation;
- immutable source comparison;
- automated identifier, link, count, and hash checks;
- dual confirmation of exact Founder wording;
- expanded evidence and contrary-view disclosure;
- narrower validation outcome;
- conditional gate treatment;
- recorded Founder risk acceptance;
- recusal from the affected activity.

Automation may support objective checks but does not itself supply independence or authority.

## 8. Recusal and Substitution

A role holder must recuse when:

- the conflict cannot be mitigated proportionately;
- impartial performance is reasonably doubtful;
- access restrictions prevent valid performance;
- the role holder would be the sole preparer and sole validator of a critical artefact;
- the Founder or Validator directs recusal within their authority.

The Review Administrator records the vacancy and initiates temporary or replacement assignment. The affected gate remains blocked until required capacity is restored.

## 9. Escalation Rules

Escalate to the Review Administrator for:

- routine assignment conflict;
- vacancy or scheduling impact;
- needed substitution.

Escalate to the Validator for:

- independence sufficiency;
- compensating-control validation;
- artefact reliance restrictions.

Escalate to the Founder for:

- high-risk role combinations;
- unresolved conflicts;
- residual risk acceptance;
- exceptions affecting Charter controls;
- conflict involving the Review Administrator or Validator.

No role holder may approve their own conflict exception.

## 10. Conflict Lifecycle

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Disclosed | Conflict recorded; no reliance decision yet |
| Under Assessment | Mitigation and authority under review |
| Mitigated | Controls implemented and validated |
| Conditionally Accepted | Founder accepts residual procedural risk with limits |
| Recused | Role holder removed from affected activity |
| Resolved | Conflict no longer applies; audit record retained |
| Reopened | New information requires reassessment |

## 11. Audit Requirements

Every conflict record must preserve:

- original disclosure;
- assessment;
- mitigation;
- decision authority;
- affected artefacts and dates;
- revalidation;
- final state;
- subsequent reopening.

Conflict records must not be deleted when an assignment ends.
