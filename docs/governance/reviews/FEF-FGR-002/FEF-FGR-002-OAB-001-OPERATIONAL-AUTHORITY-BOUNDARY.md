# FEF-FGR-002-OAB-001 — Operational Authority Boundary

## 1. Purpose

Separate operational work from governance recommendation, validation, Founder decision authority, and constitutional authority.

## 2. Authority Layers

| Layer | Nature | Permitted Actors | Permitted Effect | Prohibited Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operational Administration | Coordination, registers, sequence, scope, records, custody, preparation | Assigned operational roles | Creates and maintains controlled operational records | Cannot decide governance |
| Governance Analysis and Recommendation | Evidence-based analysis, options, candidate RQs and GFs | Review Analyst and authorised preparers | Creates candidate inputs for validation and review | Cannot create Founder authority |
| Validation Authority | Tests evidence, completeness, traceability, boundaries, lifecycle, and gate readiness | Assigned Validator, jointly with Evidence Custodian where required | Passes, conditions, returns, or blocks a validation gate | Cannot answer governance questions |
| Founder Decision Authority | Issues exact attributable Founder Decisions and reserved dispositions | Founder only | Creates an FD within its explicit scope and treatment | Cannot be exercised by implication or operational role |
| Constitutional Authority | Creates or amends constitutional effect through a separately authorised process | Authority established by the future constitutional process; Founder where expressly reserved | May create constitutional treatment only through that process | Cannot arise from this mobilisation task, GF, recommendation, or candidate |

## 3. Operational Administration

Operational administration includes:

- register control;
- RQ drafting coordination and admission administration;
- evidence custody;
- Evidence Pack preparation;
- session preparation;
- record production;
- dependency, risk, and gate management;
- completion-package assembly.

Operational administration may stop work for a failed control. It may not:

- select a governance outcome;
- infer Founder intent;
- waive reserved authority;
- declare constitutional effect;
- amend FEF-RGS-000;
- authorise Engineering Discovery.

## 4. Governance Recommendation

A recommendation:

- is attributable to its preparer;
- identifies evidence and limitations;
- remains distinct from a GF and FD;
- may present options without selecting the Founder’s option;
- has no approval or activation effect.

Repeated recommendation, apparent consensus, or lack of objection does not create a decision.

## 5. Validation Authority

Validation authority permits the Validator to:

- test mandatory controls;
- identify defects;
- record a validation outcome;
- return an artefact;
- block reliance or gate progression;
- require correction and revalidation;
- escalate reserved matters.

A validation pass means the tested record satisfies its validation criteria. It does not mean:

- the governance conclusion is approved;
- the Founder has decided;
- a standard is active;
- a constitutional principle exists;
- risk is accepted unless the Founder expressly accepts it.

## 6. Founder Decision Authority

Only the Founder may:

- issue, condition, defer, supersede, or withdraw an FD;
- approve the exact authoritative wording;
- accept a risk reserved to the Founder;
- close an Open Question through an attributable FD;
- approve final review treatment.

Operational roles shall not:

- sign, label, or present their output as a Founder Decision;
- select an option on behalf of the Founder;
- treat a Founder’s participation in operational work as an implicit decision;
- paraphrase discussion as authoritative wording;
- implement a proposed decision before it is issued.

## 7. Constitutional Authority

FEF-FGR-002 may identify Constitutional Candidates only after the approved evidence, GF, and FD prerequisites exist.

A Constitutional Candidate:

- is not constitutional text;
- is not approved constitutional authority;
- cannot amend the Constitution;
- requires separate consolidation and approval authority.

No operational role, including the Founder acting solely in an operational capacity, may create constitutional effect through an operational record.

## 8. Capacity Labelling

Every attributable action involving a person holding multiple roles must identify the capacity used, for example:

- “Founder — issuing FD”;
- “Founder acting as Review Administrator — coordinating the gate”;
- “Review Recorder — preserving exact wording”;
- “Validator — validating the decision record.”

Capacity labelling does not cure an independence defect; the conflict rules and compensating controls still apply.

## 9. Escalation Boundary

Escalate to the Founder when:

- reserved authority is required or unclear;
- scope expansion is proposed;
- an exception would affect a Charter control;
- an Open Question closure is proposed;
- risk acceptance is required;
- constitutional treatment is disputed.

Escalate to the Validator when:

- record completeness, lifecycle, traceability, or gate readiness is defective.

Escalate to the Evidence Custodian and Validator when:

- provenance, integrity, admissibility, pack version, or evidence conflict is defective.

## 10. Non-Delegation Rule

Assignment to an operational role is not delegation of Founder authority.

No assignment, RACI designation, role combination, temporary vacancy arrangement, tool capability, or coding-agent instruction may be interpreted as permission to exercise Founder or constitutional authority.

## 11. Current Boundary

No individual assignments exist. No operational role is currently empowered to begin substantive D1 review work.
