### Conclusion

Governance records, transitions, and outputs across FEF-FGR-002 receive
assurance through a sequence of layered, separately-timed passes —
evidence registration, evidence validation, session entry, session
opening reconfirmation, and post-session output validation — rather than
a single checkpoint, consistently across the examined D1 and D2 record
sets. Within that pattern, one evidence-bounded proportionality mechanism
is operated (evidence-class tiering: Admitted / Conditionally Admitted /
Context Only, restricting permitted use by class). A second dimension —
whether a transition purports to exercise or admit into Founder-reserved
authority — is not evidenced as having a distinct, heavier control tier;
the same disclosure-and-compensating-control template recurs regardless
of a transition's proximity to Founder authority. The one operated
instance in the mapped evidence where this mattered (EV-058) shows a
Founder-authority-adjacent transition (a DG-2-equivalent admission)
proceeding through the standard operational-capacity sequence, passing
its deterministic checks, and being distinguishable from a properly
authorised transition only by the presence or absence of a distinct,
non-self-generated Founder-origin input.

### Supporting Evidence

- EV-024, EV-025, EV-028 — layered D1 assurance passes (registration,
  validation, entry gate).
- EV-050, EV-052, EV-053 — layered D2 assurance passes (mobilisation gate
  sequence, session-opening reconfirmation, post-session validation).
- EV-058 — the one operated case of a Founder-authority-adjacent
  transition (DG-2-equivalent admission) proceeding without a distinct
  Founder-origin input, undetected by the deterministic checks, and found
  only through a separate non-routine investigation.

### Contrary Evidence

EV-050's evidence-class tiering (17 Admitted / 3 Conditionally Admitted /
1 Context Only) shows that proportionality **is** operated along the
evidence-admissibility dimension. This finding does not claim an absence
of proportionality generally; it distinguishes the evidenced
evidence-admissibility tiering from the unevidenced transition-authority
tiering.
