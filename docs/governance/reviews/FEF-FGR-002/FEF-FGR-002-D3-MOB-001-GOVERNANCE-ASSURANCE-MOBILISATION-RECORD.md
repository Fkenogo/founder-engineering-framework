## 3. D3 Purpose and Assurance Boundary

D3 will examine the governance controls required to provide attributable,
proportionate, and trustworthy assurance over:

- review preparation and gate readiness;
- evidence mobilisation and permitted reliance;
- substantive examination and evidence-bounded reasoning;
- Governance Finding preparation and validation;
- exact recording and implementation reliance for Founder Decisions;
- Constitutional Candidate and Deferred Matter treatment;
- domain and review closure; and
- downstream reliance on validated governance outputs.

Governance assurance is distinct from:

| Adjacent Function | D3 Boundary |
|---|---|
| Governance authority | D1 determines bounded authority; assurance cannot create or expand it |
| Evidence governance | D2 qualifies and controls evidence; assurance tests reliance and process without changing evidence meaning |
| Evidence custody | Custody preserves the chain; it does not decide assurance conclusions |
| Founder decision-making | Assurance may validate record integrity but cannot validate, simulate, or replace Founder judgement |
| Document administration | Administrative accuracy supports assurance but does not itself establish substantive validity |
| Engineering testing | Project and engineering assurance standards remain outside this governance-domain mobilisation |

## 4. Provisional Mobilisation Themes

The following are preparation themes only. They are not Review Questions, have
no identifiers or lifecycle state, and do not predetermine an answer.

### 4.1 Validation Scope and Objects

- records, transitions, outputs, and lifecycle stages requiring validation;
- identity, authority, scope, evidence, reasoning, traceability, conditions,
  register, and repository-integrity checks;
- stage-specific validation requirements.

### 4.2 Assurance Levels and Proportionality

- assurance proportionate to governance risk;
- ordinary and high-risk decisions;
- deterministic checks versus interpretive or independent review;
- minimum assurance before downstream reliance.

### 4.3 Independence and Role Combination

- definition and degree of independence;
- acceptable and prohibited combinations;
- disclosure and compensating controls;
- mandatory independent-revalidation triggers.

### 4.4 Challenge, Rebuttal, and Dissent

- duties and rights to challenge evidence and reasoning;
- attributable competing views and unresolved disagreement;
- protection against preference-based resolution;
- escalation to the Founder where reserved authority is required.

### 4.5 Validation Verdicts and Consequences

- the need for controlled pass, conditional, fail, return, stop, reopen,
  invalidate, supersede, and reliance treatments;
- the relationship between a verdict, corrective action, and downstream use.

No final vocabulary or lifecycle effect is established by this mobilisation.

### 4.6 Residual Governance Risk

- identification and recording of residual risk;
- authority to accept it;
- conditions, expiry, and reassessment triggers;
- separation of a validation pass from risk acceptance.

### 4.7 Assurance of Founder Decisions

- pre-disposition input validation and post-disposition exact-record
  validation;
- exact-directive fidelity, conditions, non-effects, and downstream reliance;
- the prohibition on automated Founder judgement or approval.

### 4.8 Assurance Continuity

- handover, reassignment, loss of Validator availability, reproducibility, and
  later challenge;
- circumstances requiring independent revalidation of a prior output.

### 4.9 Tool-Assisted and AI-Assisted Validation

- reproducible deterministic checks and their limits;
- human review and material-ambiguity escalation;
- AI-agent role boundaries, false-assurance risk, and prohibited automated
  approval.

### 4.10 Assurance Traceability

- validation input, method, checks, result, conditions, and authority;
- affected outputs, reopening triggers, and downstream reliance.
