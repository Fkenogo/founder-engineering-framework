## 3. RQ-002 — Reserved Founder Authority

**Applicable evidence:** EV-001–EV-008, EV-011, EV-015–EV-021.

**Evidence relevance:** Founder decision records reserve commencement and exact decision authority. The Charter and operational controls reserve FDs, conditions, risk acceptance, final review treatment, role-combination approval, and specified escalations. **Fact observations:**

**Traceability:** Candidate GF-002; OQ-004, OQ-008, and OQ-012 remain open.

## 4. RQ-003 — Constitutional Authority Boundary

**Applicable evidence:** EV-001–EV-015.

**Evidence relevance:** Founder decisions and architectural records repeatedly disclaim constitutional effect. The Charter permits only later candidate identification after specified prerequisites. EV-013 records an unresolved relationship between RGS and a future Constitution.

**Fact observations:**

- current Founder decisions approve review instruments within explicit scope;

**Interpretation:** Current authority operates before a Constitution and must not be mistaken for constitutional law. The pack supports a separation boundary, but not the future precedence, inheritance, or amendment model.

**Contrary evidence and limitations:** Strategic references to a future framework family and draft RGS lifecycle language are not constitutional authority.

**Uncertainty:** The future relationship among Founder authority, constitutional authority, framework standards, and domain standards is unresolved.

**Dependencies:** RQ-001, RQ-005, D7, and later attributable Founder decisions.

**Traceability:** Candidate GF-003; OQ-013 remains open.

## 5. RQ-004 — Delegated Governance

**Applicable evidence:** EV-001–EV-008, EV-011, EV-016–EV-021.

**Evidence relevance:** The Charter and operational controls distinguish role assignment from authority delegation. The Role Assignment Register demonstrates assigned functions and explicit limitations, not transferred Founder authority.

**Fact observations:**

- operational assignments are attributable, scoped, dated, and controlled;
- no assignment permits an FD, risk acceptance, OQ closure, or constitutional effect;
- role combination does not combine authority;
- no evidence item establishes a general FEF delegation instrument.

**Interpretation:** Governance delegation, if permitted later, must be express and bounded; operational assignment cannot serve as implied delegation. The evidence supports necessary control attributes but does not approve a general delegation model.

**Contrary evidence and limitations:** The current multi-role operating arrangement shows practical assignment flexibility but cannot be generalized into delegation authority.

**Uncertainty:** Permitted delegation subjects, duration, supervision, withdrawal, succession, and audit requirements remain for Founder decision.

**Dependencies:** RQ-001, RQ-002, and a future authority decision.

**Traceability:** Candidate GF-004; OQ-002 remains open in its broader governance-role application.

## 7. RQ-006 — Separation of Operational, Validation, and Decision Authority

**Applicable evidence:** EV-005–EV-008 and EV-016–EV-021.

**Evidence relevance:** The Charter, execution rules, RACI, role standard, boundary document, conflict rules, procedure, and assignments define both the intended separation and the actual resource-constrained combination.

**Fact observations:**

- operational administration, analysis, recording, custody, validation, and Founder decisions are distinct functions;
- the Codex agent currently holds five operational roles;
- preparation and validation for the same artefacts are not independent;
- disclosures, capacity labels, hashes, sequential passes, and later revalidation are compensating controls;
- compensation does not create independence or Founder authority.

**Interpretation:** The conceptual authority separation is clear, but personnel independence is limited in the current operating model. The present model can support traceable review preparation and analysis only with the limitation carried into reliance decisions.

**Contrary evidence and limitations:** Some operational source metadata still states that no assignments exist. EV-021 is the later controlling assignment record. The same-agent validation cannot establish independent assurance.

**Uncertainty:** The enduring minimum independence requirement and when independent revalidation becomes mandatory remain undecided.

**Dependencies:** Founder consideration of residual procedural risk; possible later independent validation; D3 assurance work.

**Traceability:** Candidate GF-006; OQ-002 and OQ-003 remain open.

## 8. RQ-007 — Governance Lifecycle Transition Authority

**Applicable evidence:** EV-001–EV-013 and EV-015–EV-021.

**Evidence relevance:** The decisions and execution controls distinguish drafting, approval, commencement, admission, freeze, opening, validation, disposition, and final review treatment. Draft RGS records proposed standard lifecycle states without adopting them.

**Fact observations:**

- Charter, Agenda, and Plan approval did not commence the review;
- commencement required FD-2026-07-24-009;
- RQ admission, pack freeze, session opening, FD issuance, domain exit, and final review treatment have distinct gates;
- validation does not constitute approval;
- no approved FEF-wide lifecycle transition map is present.

**Interpretation:** Explicit authority and attributable records are required for material governance transitions; status must not be inferred from completion or validation. Existing evidence is sufficient for the review lifecycle but incomplete for all FEF artefact classes.

**Contrary evidence and limitations:** Draft RGS lifecycle language and administrative status labels are proposals or bounded records, not an adopted universal lifecycle.

**Uncertainty:** Approval-versus-activation separation, effective dates, withdrawal, supersession, and owners across artefact classes remain undecided.

**Dependencies:** RQ-001 through RQ-003, D5, and future Founder decisions.

**Traceability:** Candidate GF-007; OQ-004, OQ-014, OQ-021, and OQ-022 remain open.

## 9. RQ-008 — Exceptions, Escalation, and Risk Acceptance Authority

**Applicable evidence:** EV-001–EV-008, EV-011, and EV-016–EV-021.

**Evidence relevance:** Review controls permit Validators to return, condition, or block gates; they escalate reserved authority, Charter exceptions, unresolved conflicts, and residual risk to the Founder.

**Fact observations:**

- operational roles may stop or escalate but may not accept reserved risk;
- only the Founder may accept residual procedural risk or issue an FD;
- a Charter-affecting exception requires Founder authority;
- no approved framework-wide exception or deviation mechanism appears in the pack.

**Interpretation:** The review-specific model separates control enforcement from risk acceptance. It does not settle the FEF-wide authority, expiry, review, and audit model for exceptions.

**Contrary evidence and limitations:** The current non-independent validation condition remains disclosed; it has not been converted into a general exception or silently accepted as framework policy.

**Uncertainty:** Which exceptions may be delegated, who owns routine escalations, and what expiry or renewal controls apply remain unresolved.

**Dependencies:** RQ-001, RQ-002, D3, D5, and future Founder disposition.

**Traceability:** Candidate GF-008; OQ-008 and OQ-012 remain open.
