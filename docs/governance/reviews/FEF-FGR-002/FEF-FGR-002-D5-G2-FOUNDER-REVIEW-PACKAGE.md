## 1. RQ and Evidence Boundary

### Exact Review Question

> What drafting, review, and operational states, if any, do FEF governance
> instruments pass through, and by what Founder or delegated authority is
> each state transition confirmed?

This finding uses only EV-005, EV-007, EV-008, EV-013, EV-072, EV-074,
EV-075, EV-076, and EV-077 as frozen for RQ-032 in EP-005 v2.0. EV-013 and
EV-072 remain Conditionally Admitted. EV-072 is used only at the historical
RQ-032 acquisition state. No unmapped evidence or later live administrative
version was used.

## 2. Finding

The mapped evidence establishes three bounded kinds of instrument-state
example:

1. EV-013 records its own pre-approval combination of `Draft v0.2`, `Founder
   Review Required`, and `Not Approved`. Its unapproved content does not
   establish policy.
2. EV-074 records one attributable Founder milestone disposition of `Approve
   with Conditions`, followed by separately recorded validation.
3. EV-075 and EV-076 record one domain-mobilisation transition in which
   preparation did not create operative effect and an attributable Founder
   `Approve with Conditions` disposition confirmed `Mobilised — Effective`,
   subject to four conditions.

EV-005, EV-007, EV-008, and EV-077 show that operational roles, gate outcomes,
validation results, and session states are separately controlled. EV-072
shows varied status words in repository practice, but its mutable,
non-authoritative status prevents those labels from becoming an approved
model merely through listing or repetition.

The mapped evidence therefore supports a bounded conclusion: the Founder is
the evidenced confirming authority for the two examined approval/effect
transitions, and preparation or role assignment is not itself that
confirmation. It does not evidence a complete FEF-wide lifecycle, a universal
ordering or equivalence of status words, or a delegated non-Founder authority
confirming an instrument-level transition.

## 3. Evidence Gaps and Unsupported Matters

| Matter | Finding treatment |
|---|---|
| Approved common instrument lifecycle/status vocabulary | Not evidenced; no model adopted |
| Universal ordering or equivalence of Draft, Review Required, Approved, Active, Mobilised, Effective, Closed, Withdrawn, and Superseded | Unsupported |
| Operated instrument-level Withdrawn or Superseded transition | Not evidenced |
| Delegated non-Founder confirmation of an instrument-level state transition | Not evidenced; absence is not read as prohibition |
| Assignment of RA-002–RA-006 roles as delegation of Founder authority | Unsupported; functional assignment remains distinct from authority delegation |
| Completeness or sufficiency of labels listed in EV-072 | Unsupported because the index is mutable and non-authoritative |

These gaps are part of the answer at finding level; they are not silently
filled by inference.

## 4. Contrary and Qualifying Evidence

No mapped source directly contradicts the bounded examples. Four
qualifications prevent overstatement:

- EV-013 is Not Approved and supports only its own status header;
- EV-072 is a mutable, non-authoritative index whose source records control;
- `Approve` appears in different control contexts without evidence that the
  acts are equivalent; and
- EV-074 and EV-075/EV-076 are one milestone and one domain-mobilisation
  example, not a complete lifecycle corpus.

## 5. Limitations, Uncertainty, and Open Questions

- Confidence is high that the bounded examples and their attributable Founder
  authority are accurately represented.
- Confidence is moderate that the mapped evidence demonstrates a deliberate
  separation among instrument, gate, assurance, and session state axes.
- No positive confidence is assigned to a universal lifecycle, vocabulary,
  sequencing rule, or delegated-authority model because the evidence does not
  establish one.
- OQ-004 and OQ-022 remain open and unchanged. This finding neither answers nor
  dispositions them.
- The same acting capacity performed analysis, recording, and validation;
  validation is non-independent.

## 1. RQ and Evidence Boundary

### Exact Review Question

> When a FEF governance instrument is approved, amended, or withdrawn, does
> it apply retrospectively to work already underway, only to work commenced
> after its effective date, or under some other transitional rule, and who
> decides which rule applies?

This finding uses only EV-009, EV-010, EV-012, EV-013, EV-070, EV-073,
EV-078, and EV-079 as frozen for RQ-033 in EP-005 v2.0. EV-013 remains
Conditionally Admitted. EV-078 remains correction/recovery-only and is never
treated as an ordinary amendment. No unmapped evidence or later live version
was used.

## 2. Finding

The mapped evidence establishes four bounded treatments relevant to
applicability and transition:

1. EV-009 records scoped Founder architectural decisions as current
   constraints and direction while assigning no effective status or effective
   date and supplying no approval date.
2. EV-010 records a Founder-approved pilot whose commencement is tied to
   completion and validation of a named work package. That is one
   event-triggered, pilot-specific treatment, not a universal rule.
3. EV-078 records a Founder-authorised recovery that restored active controls
   to the last authorised boundary, quarantined unauthorised work, and treated
   the affected identifiers as not currently allocated while preserving the
   material for audit and possible later reuse.
4. EV-079 records an explicit correction that superseded false closure and
   authority claims for active reliance while preserving the original version
   in repository history.

EV-070 requires version, authority, limitation, and purpose traceability
through later outputs and decisions; EV-073 demonstrates one version-stamped
D3 traceability chain. These controls support preservation of what governed a
decision, but they do not select a transitional rule.

The evidence therefore supports a bounded conclusion: FEF practice contains
decision-specific commencement and defect-correction treatments, and the
Founder is the evidenced authority behind the mapped architectural and
recovery examples. It does not establish a general retrospective,
prospective, hybrid, or grandfathering rule for valid work underway when an
instrument is approved, ordinarily amended, or withdrawn. It also does not
establish delegated non-Founder authority to select such a rule.

## 3. Evidence Gaps and Unsupported Matters

| Matter | Finding treatment |
|---|---|
| Approved general transitional-applicability rule | Not evidenced; no rule adopted |
| Default treatment of valid work already underway | Unsupported |
| Ordinary amendment or withdrawal of a valid effective instrument with explicit transitional treatment | Not evidenced; correction examples not substituted |
| General distinction among approval, record, decision, and effective dates | Not evidenced |
| Prospective-only inference from absence of retrospective wording | Unsupported |
| Delegated non-Founder rule-selection authority | Not evidenced; absence is not read as prohibition |
| Application of a transition rule to FEF-RGS-000 | Prohibited and unsupported; the instrument remains Not Approved with no effective date |

These gaps are part of the answer at finding level and remain visible rather
than being filled by preference or extrapolation.

## 4. Contrary and Qualifying Evidence

No mapped source directly contradicts the bounded examples. Four
qualifications prevent overstatement:

- EV-009's current-constraint treatment does not say how prior or underway
  work is affected.
- EV-010's commencement trigger is scoped to one pilot and contains no
  retrospective or grandfathering treatment.
- EV-078 and EV-079 concern invalid or inaccurate prior states, not ordinary
  amendment or withdrawal of a valid instrument.
- EV-013 is Not Approved and its proposed content has no current authority.

## 5. Limitations, Uncertainty, and OQ-017

- Confidence is high that the four bounded examples and their recorded
  authority/scope are accurately represented.
- Confidence is moderate that the examples demonstrate decision-specific

- No positive confidence is assigned to a universal rule, a default for work
  underway, or delegated rule selection because the mapped evidence does not

- OQ-017 remains open, unchanged, and undispositioned. Its mapping is clarified

## 1. RQ and Evidence Boundary

### Exact Review Question

> What versioning and release-state conventions, if any, should apply
> uniformly across FEF governance instruments, and how does this relate to
> the version-history practice already separately operated by the Master
> Programme and individual review records?

This finding uses only EV-012, EV-013, EV-072, EV-073, EV-080, and EV-081 as
frozen for RQ-034 in EP-005 v2.0. EV-013 and EV-072 remain Conditionally
Admitted. EV-072, EV-080, and EV-081 are bounded to their governed historical
RQ-034 acquisitions; no later live version or unmapped evidence was used.

## 2. Finding

The mapped evidence establishes multiple traceable practices:

1. EV-080 operates an append-only Master Programme `v0.NN` revision history,
   but states no rule for digit meaning, increment significance, reset, reuse,
   or application to other artefact classes.
2. EV-073 demonstrates a register with its own overall version distinct from
   the versions and states of traced records. EV-081 demonstrates a related
   dual-axis pattern: an overall Register version coexists with independent
   per-RQ Version fields.
3. EV-013 records an instrument-level `Draft v0.2` separately from Status,
   Approval status, Effective date, and Authority. It is Not Approved and
   cannot supply an approved convention.
4. EV-072 records these and other version/status notations together in one
   mutable, non-authoritative index. The source records, not the index, control.
5. EV-012 records OQ-014 as an open Founder governance decision concerning the
   versioning, release-state, effective-date, and withdrawal model for research
   standards.

These practices are partially consistent in making change and state
traceable, but the evidence does not establish an approved uniform convention
or a rule reconciling programme, register, per-entry, and instrument axes. The
coexistence of unreconciled practices creates a material coordination and
conflation risk. It does not establish that they are logically inconsistent,
nor that one observed pattern should govern all artefacts.

## 3. Evidence Gaps and Unsupported Matters

|---|---|
| Approved FEF-wide versioning or release-state convention | Not evidenced; no convention adopted |
| Meaning of major/minor or other version-number positions | Unsupported |
| Uniform increment, reset, reuse, retirement, or supersession rule | Unsupported |
| Reconciliation of programme, register, per-entry, and instrument axes | Not evidenced; preserved as a coordination gap |
| Equivalence of version, lifecycle/release state, validation verdict, effective date, and repository revision | Unsupported; dimensions remain distinct |
| Renumbering of existing instruments | Unsupported and outside this finding |

## 4. Contrary and Qualifying Evidence

EV-013 is a non-authoritative draft; EV-072 is a mutable non-authoritative
index; EV-073 and EV-081 are bounded register examples; and EV-080 demonstrates
operation without defining a universal convention. These qualifications
prevent recurrence from being treated as approval or sufficiency.
