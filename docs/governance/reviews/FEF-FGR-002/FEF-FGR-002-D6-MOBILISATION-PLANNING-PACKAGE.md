## 1. Purpose and Boundary

This package gives the Founder a lean basis for deciding whether and how to
authorise later D6 mobilisation. It is limited to **residual cross-project
Framework Administration** after D1–D5 closure.

The Charter defines D6 as identifiers, taxonomy, classifications, roadmap
relationships, simplicity, and administrative controls, without implicit
programme-architecture redesign. The Agenda and Plan add register, document,
roadmap, work-package, and minimum-viable-administration concerns. This package
applies those boundaries after removing matters already settled by D1–D5.

This is planning only. It does not mobilise D6, create a formal Review Question
Candidate Set, allocate an RQ identifier, prepare an RQ, or trigger DG-2.

## 2. Residual Cross-Project Administrative Matters

The following residual matters may justify a narrow D6 if the Founder later
authorises mobilisation:

1. minimum ownership and stewardship responsibilities for framework-wide
   records whose maintenance crosses Founder projects;
2. minimum administrative maintenance responsibilities for keeping current
   programme and governance controls usable and traceable;
3. roadmap, work-package, release, and dependency administration where a
   relationship is genuinely cross-project rather than project-specific;
4. operational stewardship boundaries between framework administration,
   project authority, Founder-reserved authority, and separately governed
   implementation;
5. minimum control-maintenance and reporting responsibilities needed to expose
   stale state, unresolved dependencies, and authoritative current versions;
6. residual identifier, register, classification, and document-relationship
   ambiguity not already settled by D1, D4, or D5; and
7. proportionality and tailoring needed to keep cross-project administration
   minimal.

No matter is included merely to complete the domain numbering sequence.

## 3. Explicit Exclusions

D6 mobilisation planning and any later D6 scope must not:

- reopen, reinterpret, or repeat D1–D5 findings or decisions;
- implement FD-026 through FD-038 or convert accepted directions into
  operating controls;
- create a universal lifecycle, versioning, transition, exception, or legacy
  model;
- determine constitutional placement or commence D7;
- resolve an Open Question or commence D8 final synthesis;
- create the final Open Question Traceability Matrix;
- activate, evaluate, disposition, or implement Framework Evolution,
  FEF-FEV-001-FEC-001, FEF-CCF-001, FAP-001, or CE1–CE6;
- create project-specific repository, roadmap, delivery, or release rules;
- create Constitutional Candidates or Deferred Matters during this planning
  activity; or
- commence final assembly, consolidation, RGS amendment, or implementation.

## 10. Proposed Mobilisation Conditions

If the Founder later authorises D6 mobilisation, the following conditions are
proposed for consideration without preselection:

1. limit D6 to residual administration that is demonstrably cross-project;
2. treat D1–D5 findings, decisions, conditions, and non-effects as protected
   predecessor controls, not topics for reconsideration;
3. require separate post-mobilisation preparation and Founder review before
   any formal Review Question is admitted;
4. preserve the 23 Open Questions, 0/0 Candidate/Deferred state, and blocked
   FEF-P0-004 treatment unless separately authorised;
5. exclude implementation, Framework Evolution, D7, D8 final synthesis, and
   final assembly; and
6. prefer one proportionate examination unit unless admitted scope and evidence
   later demonstrate a need for more.

These are proposed mobilisation conditions only. They are not selected,
effective, or binding before a separate Founder decision.

## 11. Proportionate Execution Approach

A proportionate later D6 route would:

1. mobilise the narrow residual scope only after separate Founder authority;
2. prepare provisional themes into formal RQ material only after mobilisation;
3. submit any formal wording for Founder review and DG-2 admission under the
   existing methodology;
4. identify only evidence needed to test genuinely cross-project
   administrative needs;
5. use one examination unit unless later governed preparation demonstrates
   otherwise; and
6. defer operating design and implementation until separately authorised after
   any Founder Decisions.
