## 2. Preparation Method

The Agenda §6.5 identifies nine strategic-examination items for D5: (1)
drafting and review states; (2) approval versus operational treatment; (3)
applicability and transition; (4) versioning; (5) amendment; (6) supersession
and withdrawal; (7) exceptions and expiry; (8) controlled evolution; (9)
treatment of legacy governance. The Review Analyst:

1. treated these nine items as coverage inputs rather than a target question count, consistent with the D3 precedent;
2. combined items 1 and 2 (drafting/review states; approval versus operational treatment) because both concern one indivisible question — what states an instrument passes through and who confirms each transition;
3. kept item 3 (applicability and transition) separate because it concerns the *effect* of a change on work already underway, a distinct question from the state model itself;
4. kept item 4 (versioning) separate because a numbering/release convention is examinable independently of the status vocabulary;
5. combined items 5 and 6 (amendment; supersession and withdrawal) because both concern the mechanics and ownership of ending or changing an instrument's current form;
6. combined items 7 and 8 (exceptions and expiry; controlled evolution) because both concern bounded, time-limited, or evolutionary departure from a static baseline, distinguished from item 5–6's permanent change; and
7. kept item 9 (treatment of legacy governance) separate because it is a backward-looking classification question distinct from any forward-looking lifecycle mechanism.

No candidate states a preferred status vocabulary, versioning scheme,
approval threshold, or lifecycle-model design.

## 5. Proportionality and Overlap Assessment

Six candidates are proportionate because:

- themes 1 and 2 are one indivisible status/approval question;
- theme 3 remains separate from themes 5–6 because transitional *effect* on existing work is a distinct question from the *mechanics* of the change itself;
- theme 4 remains separate from theme 1 because a numbering convention is examinable independently of the state vocabulary;
- themes 5 and 6 combine because both concern permanent change to an instrument's current form and its ownership;
- themes 7 and 8 combine because both concern bounded or evolutionary departure from a static baseline, distinguished from themes 5–6's permanent change; and
- theme 9 remains separate because it is a backward-looking classification question, not a forward-looking mechanism.

No merge or removal is proposed. The separately labelled Validator pass may
return any candidate for revision before canonical identifier allocation or
DG-2 admission.
