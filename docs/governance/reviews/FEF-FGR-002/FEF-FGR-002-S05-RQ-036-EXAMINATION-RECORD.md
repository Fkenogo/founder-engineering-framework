## 3. Examination Results by Analytical Classification

### 3.1 Directly Established by Evidence

1. EV-005 establishes one approved, Charter-specific exception rule. Any
   exception to the Charter must identify the affected clause, reason, scope,
   duration, risks, compensating controls, approval authority, and expiry or
   review trigger; it cannot delegate Founder decision authority or permit
   evidence reconstruction.
2. EV-012 establishes that OQ-012 remains an open governance question asking
   what exception, deviation, expiry, and escalation mechanism applies to
   research standards.
3. EV-017 establishes that Founder Decision Authority belongs to the Founder
   only and cannot arise through implication or operational role. It also
   separates administration, analysis, validation, and Founder decision
   capacities.
4. EV-066 records one attributable Accept with Conditions Founder Decision,
   with exact conditions and exclusions but without invoking a named clause,
   duration, expiry trigger, exception, deviation, or waiver.
5. EV-074 records one attributable Approve with Conditions milestone decision
   and expressly routes future methodology improvements prospectively through
   Framework Evolution rather than retrospective alteration of closed work.
   It does not itself perform Framework Evolution or evaluate a candidate.
6. EV-083 establishes an active Framework Evolution intake mechanism whose
   authority is intake only. It expressly does not amend the framework, approve
   or reject candidates, or perform Framework Evolution.

### 3.2 Reasonably Supported

1. The evidence reasonably supports treating a clause-specific exception and
   a framework-change intake as different governed operations: the former is a
   bounded departure under the Charter's own rule, while the latter is a route
   for considering prospective framework change. No source elevates this
   observed distinction into a general FEF-wide boundary test.
2. The evidence reasonably supports distinguishing conditioned dispositions
   from proven exceptions. EV-066 and EV-074 attach conditions to decisions,
   but neither contains the Charter §21.3 metadata required for a
   clause-specific exception.
3. The evidence reasonably supports separating record preparation,
   administration, evidence custody, and validation from Founder Decision
   Authority. It does not establish who may approve an exception that is not
   itself a Founder Decision.

### 3.3 Unsupported

The mapped evidence does not support:

- a general FEF-wide exception, deviation, waiver, or expiry mechanism;
- an approved test separating every bounded exception from controlled
  evolution;
- any delegated exception authority;
- automatic classification of Accept with Conditions or Approve with
  Conditions dispositions as exceptions;
- general duration, expiry, renewal, revocation, monitoring, closure,
  escalation, ownership, traceability, or exception-register mechanics;
- automatic enforcement when an exception duration or review trigger is met;
- any exception or deviation applying to FEF-RGS-000;
- evaluation, disposition, adoption, activation, or effectiveness of
  FEF-FEV-001-FEC-001, FEF-CCF-001, CE1–CE6, or another candidate; or
- commencement of D6 or D7.

### 3.4 Contradictory or Qualifying Matters

No mapped source directly imposes an incompatible rule. The material issue is
unreconciled scope:

- EV-005's rule is expressly a rule for exceptions to the Charter, not every
  FEF governance instrument;
- EV-012 poses the exception/expiry question for research standards and does
  not answer it;
- EV-017 controls Founder Decision Authority but does not classify all
  exception approvals as Founder Decisions;
- EV-066 and EV-074 show conditioned dispositions but not clause-invoking,
  time-bounded exceptions;
- EV-083 establishes intake availability but no substantive definition,
  evaluation, or adoption threshold for controlled evolution; and
- no source reconciles the Charter rule, conditioned-disposition pattern, and
  Framework Evolution intake into one approved model.

### 3.5 Uncertain

It remains uncertain whether any general exception mechanism should exist;
which authority may approve a non-FD exception; whether and how deviations or
waivers differ from exceptions; how duration, expiry, renewal, revocation,
monitoring, escalation, ownership, closure, and registration should work; when
a temporary departure becomes controlled evolution; and how OQ-012 should be
resolved for FEF-RGS-000. The evidence supplies no operated Charter §21.3
exception with which to test those questions.

### 3.6 Outside Scope

This loop does not grant or apply an exception, deviation, waiver, or expiry;
classify every conditioned disposition; design, propose, approve, or apply an
exception/evolution model; assign delegation or ownership; create an exception

FEF-CCF-001, CE1–CE6, or another candidate; resolve OQ-012; examine RQ-037;
