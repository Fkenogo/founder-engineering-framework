## 1. Executive Summary

**Why D6 exists.** Per the Founder-approved Charter, D6 — Framework
Administration examines "Identifiers, taxonomy, classifications, roadmap
relationships, simplicity, and administrative controls" and must "not
redesign programme architecture implicitly." It exists because, per the
Agenda, "identifiers, classifications, taxonomies, registers, and roadmap
relationships make governance usable, but excessive administration can
become a substitute for judgment."

**Why it follows D5.** The Review Plan makes D6 dependent on the outcomes of
D1 (authority), D4 (records and custodianship), and D5 (lifecycle and
versioning) — all now Closed. D6 is deliberately sequenced last among the
substantive domains before the constitutional and open-question domains
because it can only examine administrative controls once the fundamental
authority, record, and lifecycle controls those administrative controls
serve are already settled.

**Why its scope has been deliberately narrowed.** The Post-D5 Founder
disposition ([FEF-FGR-002-POST-D5-FDR-001](FEF-FGR-002-POST-D5-FOUNDER-DISPOSITION-RECORD.md))
authorised only a lean D6 mobilisation-planning package limited to
**residual cross-project Framework Administration** — matters not already
settled by D1, D4, or D5. The subsequent Founder mobilisation disposition
([FEF-FGR-002-D6-FMAR-001](FEF-FGR-002-D6-FOUNDER-MOBILISATION-AUTHORISATION-RECORD.md))
preserved that narrowing through eight explicit conditions. No matter is
included in D6 merely to complete the domain numbering sequence.

**Why D6 examines Framework Administration rather than redesigning the
Framework.** The Charter's D6 boundary is explicit: D6 "does not redesign
programme architecture implicitly." D6's purpose, as stated in the validated
Examination Architecture, is "to determine the minimum administrative
controls needed to keep the Founder Engineering Framework's identifiers,
registers, roadmap and work-package relationships, and cross-project
documentation usable, traceable, and proportionate — without redesigning
programme architecture, without repeating D1/D4/D5, and without creating new
governance authority." D6 examines whether existing controls are
proportionate; it does not design new ones by itself commissioning them.

**Why the Framework is now transitioning from construction to execution.**
The Founder's exact mobilisation rationale states: "the Framework has
matured sufficiently to transition from framework construction toward
governed execution," and Condition 8 records that "the Founder Engineering
Framework is now considered operational," with "future changes to the
Framework itself... exceptional and justified by governance necessity rather
than preference or convenience," and "execution of the approved methodology...
now the primary objective." D6 is the first domain examined entirely inside
that operational posture.

This section summarises; it makes no recommendation.

## 2. Administrative Philosophy

The following operational philosophy is observable in the closed D1–D5
record. It is presented neutrally, as description, not as a proposed
constitutional principle or standard.

| Philosophy Statement | Where This Is Observable in D1–D5 |
|---|---|
| Administration exists to preserve governance, not to replace it | D4's records/custody controls (FD-026–FD-032) exist so governance decisions remain traceable and usable, not to create new decision authority |
| Governance exists to support Founder projects, not the reverse | D1's authority controls and the Charter's project-vs-framework boundary keep the Framework in service of project outcomes rather than an end in itself |
| Administration should remain proportionate to what it protects | The Agenda assigns D6 only **Medium** priority — lower than D1–D5's Critical/High ratings — because it enables consistent operation only after the fundamental controls are already known |
| Administration should not become work for its own sake | The Agenda names "Over-governance — excess roles, states, registers, and controls reduce usability" as a named risk, mitigated by "the simplicity principle and D6 Medium priority" |
| Governance should minimise unnecessary administrative burden | D5's lifecycle controls (FD-033–FD-038) were bounded to what status/versioning/applicability genuinely required, not a universal change-management system; D6's own mobilisation was narrowed to residual matters only, not a full domain re-examination |

This philosophy is descriptive of the pattern already visible in closed
work. It is not adopted here as a constitutional principle, a governance
standard, or a Framework Evolution proposal. Any future adoption remains a
separate, explicit, Founder-gated decision.

## 4. Administrative Architecture Review

The current administrative-control architecture, adopted 2026-08-04 per the
Founder-recommended Single Source of Truth model
([FEF-MASTER-PROGRAMME.md](../../../programme/FEF-MASTER-PROGRAMME.md) §1.1),
is:

```text
Master Programme (FEF-MP-001)
        │  (single authoritative source of programme state)
        │
        ├── Founder Dashboard
        │       Navigation and executive-view document.
        │       Summarises and links to the Master Programme;
        │       does not independently maintain programme state.
        │
        ├── Review Identity (FEF-FGR-002)
        │       Holds review identity, review metadata, and
        │       controlled-register identifiers for FEF-FGR-002 only.
        │       Does not independently restate current programme-level
        │       position beyond what is intrinsic to identifying the
        │       review and its own controlled registers.
        │
        └── Document Manifest
                Registers the existence, location, and category of
                controlled artefacts. Does not independently assert
                programme status; status columns reference the
                Master Programme rather than restate it.
```

**Why the Master Programme is now the single authoritative programme-control
document.** As the Framework grew, the same programme state (current
milestone, current domain, next governed activity) was independently
maintained in the Master Programme, Dashboard, Review Identity, and Manifest.
This created unnecessary maintenance overhead and a documented risk of
divergence (corrected transparently, for example, in the Post-D5 prerequisite
that reconciled a stale `0.86`/`v0.88` header mismatch). The Founder
recommended, and this repository adopted, a single-controller model: one
document is authoritative for programme state; every other document either
consumes that state by reference or is restricted to its own distinct
constitutional purpose (review identity, artefact registration).

**Why the remaining documents consume programme state rather than
independently control it.** Each remaining document retains a genuine,
narrower purpose that is not itself "programme state": the Dashboard's
purpose is executive navigation; the Review Identity's purpose is to
identify the review and its own controlled registers; the Manifest's purpose
is to register artefact existence and location. None of these purposes
requires an independent copy of "where the programme is" — they require only
a current reference to the Master Programme.

This section presents the current architecture for Founder confirmation. It
does not propose a redesign of that architecture.

## 6. Future Administrative Standards

The following are possible future administrative standardisation areas that
may, at the Founder's later discretion, be examined within D6 or considered
separately. **These are possible future directions only. No recommendation
is made. No constitutional proposal is made. No Framework Evolution activity
is created or implied by naming them.**

| Possible Future Area | Illustrative Scope |
|---|---|
| Programme Authority Levels | Whether a formal, tiered model of "who may assert what kind of programme/review/constitutional state" would clarify the single-source-of-truth model beyond the current document-role table |
| Repository Administration | Whether minimum repository-hygiene responsibilities (navigation, link integrity, unregistered-file discovery — as already performed ad hoc in FEF-RCR-001) should become a standing, proportionate administrative practice |
| Register Administration | Whether the eight controlled registers (RQR, ER, EPR, SR, GFR, FDR, CCR, DMR) need a minimum standing maintenance or custody rule beyond current per-domain practice |
| Identifier Administration | Whether the collision-safe allocation procedure already used ad hoc for every new identifier should be documented as a minimum standing administrative rule |
| Cross-project reporting | Whether any minimum, proportionate reporting format should exist for other Founder projects that adopt FEF, without making FEF review machinery mandatory project governance |

Any future adoption of any of these areas remains a separate, explicit,
Founder-gated decision, made only through the existing Founder Governance
Review lifecycle, D6 examination, or a distinct Framework Evolution
submission — never through this list.
