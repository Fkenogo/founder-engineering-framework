# FEF-FGRC-001 — Founder Governance Review Charter

**Programme:** Founder Engineering Framework  
**Document identifier:** FEF-FGRC-001  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Founder Review Draft — Not Approved  
**Work package:** FEF-WPK-001E  
**Preparation date:** 2026-07-24  
**Approval authority:** Founder  
**Current authority effect:** None  
**Controlling effect after approval:** Controlling instrument for the separately authorised Founder Governance Review and its sessions  
**Constitutional effect:** None — the Charter does not create or amend constitutional principles  
**Standard effect:** None — the Charter does not amend or approve FEF-RGS-000

## 1. Charter Purpose

This Charter defines the governance, evidence, session, finding, decision, traceability, validation, and approval controls for a new attributable Founder Governance Review of the Founder Engineering Framework.

The new review is intended to establish a fresh authoritative governance baseline after the evidence supporting an earlier historical review was determined to be unrecoverable.

This Charter governs how the future review shall be conducted. It does not conduct the review, determine its conclusions, create a Governance Finding, issue a Founder Decision, amend the Constitution, or amend a governance standard.

### 1.1 Controlling Status

This Charter shall become the controlling document for subsequent Founder Governance Review sessions only when:

1. the Founder expressly approves this Charter;
2. the approved version and approval record are preserved;
3. the Founder assigns or approves the new review’s identifier and commencement authority;
4. the Master Programme is updated to reflect the authorised review path.

Until those conditions are met, this document remains a Founder Review Draft and no review session is authorised by it.

## 2. Authority Basis and Historical Boundary

The Founder has selected a transition path that:

- recognises the historical Founder Governance Review as an event that occurred;
- recognises that its authoritative evidence is unrecoverable for current programme purposes;
- preserves FEF-FGR-001 as a historical evidence-gap record;
- retires the missing historical evidence as the future governance dependency;
- establishes a new attributable review path.

This Charter implements only the preparatory authority supplied through FEF-WPK-001E. It does not implement programme status changes or commence the new review.

### 2.1 Historical Non-Substitution

The new review shall not:

- be described as a reconstruction of the historical Sessions 1–6;
- reproduce or infer GF-001–GF-036;
- reproduce or infer FD-001–FD-033;
- treat FEF-FGR-001 as substantive governance authority;
- claim continuity of conclusions that cannot be evidenced;
- overwrite, complete, or repair FEF-FGR-001.

The historical evidence-gap record and the future review record shall remain separate.

## 3. Review Purpose and Objectives

### 3.1 Review Purpose

The future Founder Governance Review shall examine the enduring governance questions required to establish an attributable foundation for FEF constitutional consolidation and later standards alignment.

### 3.2 Review Objectives

The review shall:

1. identify the governance questions that require Founder determination;
2. examine those questions using admissible and traceable evidence;
3. distinguish evidence, analysis, Governance Findings, recommendations, and Founder Decisions;
4. record uncertainty, disagreement, alternatives, and deferred matters;
5. obtain explicit Founder Decisions where a decision is required;
6. map every finding and decision to its evidence, session, affected Open Questions, and downstream implications;
7. identify enduring constitutional candidates without automatically creating constitutional principles;
8. establish a validated authoritative review record;
9. provide a controlled input to later constitutional consolidation;
10. preserve a complete audit trail contemporaneously.

## 4. Review Scope

### 4.1 In Scope

The future review may examine:

- FEF governance authority and reserved Founder authority;
- delegation, accountability, and decision rights;
- evidence, provenance, traceability, transparency, and explainability;
- validation, assurance, review, approval, and risk acceptance;
- records, registers, retention, archival, and information lifecycle;
- governance lifecycle, status, versioning, exceptions, applicability, and controlled change;
- framework administration, identifiers, taxonomy, classification, and simplicity;
- the boundary between constitutional principles and domain-specific standards;
- transitional governance needed before a complete Constitution exists;
- the 23 open questions associated with FEF-RGS-000;
- additional governance questions admitted through the Charter’s change control;
- potential constitutional candidates derived from new attributable findings and decisions;
- implications for later constitutional consolidation and RGS amendment.

### 4.2 Permitted Inputs

Permitted review inputs may include:

- current repository governance and programme records;
- FEF-RGS-000 Draft v0.2 as a draft review input only;
- the 23-question FEF-WPK-001 Open Questions Register;
- FAR-001 and FAR-002 within their recorded authority boundaries;
- FEF-FGR-001 as evidence of the historical gap only;
- WPK-001B.5 and WPK-001B.5A audit and recovery records;
- attributable Founder instructions issued for the new review;
- applicable external laws, standards, professional codes, or authoritative references;
- clearly labelled technical or governance analysis prepared for the new review.

Admission of a document does not elevate its authority or approve its content.

## 5. Exclusions

The review governed by this Charter shall not, unless separately authorised:

- amend, approve, activate, adopt, or assign an effective date to the FEF Constitution;
- amend, approve, activate, adopt, or assign an effective date to FEF-RGS-000;
- draft or approve FEF-RDS-001;
- commence Engineering Asset Discovery;
- create engineering implementation work packages;
- design product or project implementation requirements;
- recreate the historical Founder Governance Review;
- infer missing historical findings or decisions;
- treat an analytical recommendation as a Founder Decision;
- close an Open Question without an attributable Founder Decision;
- create another governance domain or standard;
- alter the authority of existing projects;
- implement constitutional extraction within a review session;
- authorise downstream work merely because the review is complete.

## 6. Governance Domains

The review shall cover the following domains. A domain may require one or more sessions, and a session may cover more than one domain only when the approved session plan preserves traceability.

| Domain | Review Focus | Required Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| D1 — Governance Authority | Founder authority, reserved authority, constitutional authority, delegation, accountability, transitional authority | Does not create authority before Founder decision |
| D2 — Evidence Governance | Admissibility, provenance, evidence hierarchy, traceability, transparency, explainability, uncertainty | Does not treat assertion as evidence |
| D3 — Governance Assurance | Validation, review, approval, activation, risk acceptance, exceptions, findings assurance | Does not approve standards |
| D4 — Records and Information Governance | Records, registers, retention, archival, access, information lifecycle, organisational knowledge | Does not establish implementation systems |
| D5 — Governance Lifecycle and Evolution | Status, versioning, applicability, supersession, withdrawal, controlled evolution, transitional treatment | Does not activate a lifecycle model without decision |
| D6 — Framework Administration | Identifiers, taxonomy, classifications, roadmap relationships, simplicity, administrative controls | Does not redesign programme architecture implicitly |
| D7 — Constitutional and Standards Boundary | Enduring principles, domain-specific governance, inheritance, constitutional extraction candidates | Does not amend the Constitution or RGS |
| D8 — Open Question Resolution | Existing RGS Open Questions and newly admitted governance questions | Mapping does not constitute closure |

The approved review initiation record shall map every planned session to one or more domains and shall identify any domain intentionally deferred.

## 7. Roles and Authority

| Role | Responsibilities | Authority Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Founder | Approves the Charter; authorises the review; issues Founder Decisions; approves the completed review record | Sole authority to issue or amend an FD under this Charter |
| Review Coordinator | Prepares session plan, maintains sequence, controls scope, coordinates review artefacts | Cannot issue GF conclusions unilaterally or make FDs |
| Evidence Custodian | Registers sources, preserves provenance and integrity, maintains evidence index | Cannot decide evidential meaning or governance outcomes |
| Review Analyst | Analyses admitted evidence and drafts candidate findings | Cannot convert analysis into Founder authority |
| Session Recorder | Produces the attributable session record and captures exact Founder wording | Cannot paraphrase a Founder Decision as authoritative wording |
| Validator | Validates evidence links, structure, completeness, internal consistency, and authority boundaries | Cannot resolve substantive governance questions |
| Technical Adviser | Provides bounded technical or professional advice when requested | Advice remains evidence or analysis, not a decision |
| Coding Agent | Drafts, structures, cross-references, and validates within explicit instructions | Cannot conduct Founder authority, infer decisions, or approve artefacts |

Role combinations must be recorded in the review plan. Combining roles does not combine their authority. The Founder role may not be delegated by implication.

## 9. Evidence Admissibility

### 9.1 Evidence Classes

| Class | Evidence Type | Permitted Use |
|---|---|---|
| E1 — Attributable Founder Evidence | Exact Founder instruction, decision record, approved transcript, signed or otherwise attributable disposition | May establish Founder intent or decision within its recorded scope |
| E2 — Controlled FEF Record | Versioned repository document with identity, status, provenance, and integrity metadata | May establish recorded programme, draft, review, or governance state within its authority boundary |
| E3 — External Authoritative Evidence | Applicable law, regulation, formal standard, professional code, or primary authoritative publication | May establish an external constraint or recognised practice |
| E4 — Technical Evidence | Verified engineering analysis, audit output, validation result, or reproducible technical record | May support feasibility, risk, consistency, or implementation implications |
| E5 — Expert or Advisory Analysis | Attributable advice, comparison, interpretation, or recommendation | May inform a finding but cannot independently establish Founder authority |
| C1 — Context Only | Historical assertion, unverified note, illustrative example, incomplete record, or secondary summary | May explain context; cannot support a governance conclusion without corroboration |

### 9.2 Mandatory Admissibility Tests

Before evidence may support a GF, the Evidence Custodian and Validator shall confirm:

1. **Identity:** the source is uniquely identifiable.
2. **Provenance:** origin, custodian, author or issuing authority, and acquisition route are recorded where available.
3. **Integrity:** version, date, repository state, hash, revision, or equivalent integrity indicator is recorded where proportionate.
4. **Authority:** the source’s authority and limitations are explicit.
5. **Relevance:** the source is relevant to a stated review question.
6. **Temporal applicability:** the relevant date or period is known or its absence is recorded.
7. **Accessibility:** the source can be inspected by the authorised reviewer or its access limitation is declared.
8. **Conflict treatment:** contradictory or superseding evidence is recorded.
9. **Uncertainty:** ambiguity, incompleteness, and inference are labelled.
10. **Permitted use:** the source’s use complies with confidentiality, legal, security, and repository boundaries.

Failure of an admissibility test shall result in rejection, conditional admission, or context-only treatment. The disposition and rationale must be recorded.

### 9.3 Prohibited Evidence Treatment

The review shall not:

- infer a decision from silence, absence, or programme expectation;
- use FEF-FGR-001 as evidence of historical GF or FD content;
- present AI-generated text as Founder evidence;
- hide conflicting evidence;
- convert an illustrative example into an approved rule;
- cite a document beyond its recorded authority;
- use an unavailable source without recording the access limitation;
- treat repetition of an assertion as corroboration.

## 10. Evidence Records and Traceability

Each admitted source shall have an Evidence Record containing, where applicable:

- canonical evidence identifier;
- source title and type;
- source location or stable reference;
- author, issuer, custodian, and approval authority;
- creation, issue, access, and review dates;
- version or revision;
- hash or equivalent integrity value;
- evidence class;
- authority and scope;
- confidentiality or access classification;
- relevant excerpts or precise internal references;
- related review questions, sessions, GFs, FDs, and Open Questions;
- conflicts, limitations, and uncertainty;
- admissibility disposition and validator;
- retention location.

### 10.1 Required Traceability Chain

Every substantive conclusion shall support the following chain:

```text
Evidence Record
      ↓
Review Question
      ↓
Session Record
      ↓
Governance Finding
      ↓
Founder Decision, when required
      ↓
Open Question disposition
      ↓
Constitutional candidate or domain-standard implication
      ↓
Separate downstream work package
```

A link may be marked `Not applicable`, `Deferred`, or `No decision issued` only with a recorded rationale.

## 11. Session Planning and Numbering

### 11.1 Review Plan

Before the first session, an approved Review Plan shall define:

- review identifier;
- Charter version;
- objectives and scope;
- domain coverage;
- planned sessions and sequence;
- review questions for each session;
- evidence pack for each session;
- roles and participants;
- expected artefacts;
- validation method;
- dependencies and constraints;
- change-control method;
- target completion criteria.

The Review Plan shall not pre-decide findings or decisions.

### 11.2 Session Numbering

Sessions shall use sequential canonical identifiers in the form `<REVIEW-ID>-SNN`, beginning at `S01`.

- identifiers shall be assigned before the session;
- a cancelled session number shall not be reused;
- an additional session shall receive the next number;
- a reconvened session shall receive a new number and link to the prior session;
- a continuation shall not silently replace an earlier record;
- session order shall not imply authority precedence.

### 11.3 Session Entry Gate

A session may begin only when:

1. the Charter is Founder approved;
2. the review and session identifiers are assigned;
3. the session plan is approved within the review authority;
4. the evidence pack is registered and version-frozen;
5. review questions are stated;
6. roles are assigned;
7. the prior session’s required validation is complete, unless the approved plan permits parallel sessions;
8. unresolved entry risks are disclosed.

### 11.4 Session Record

Each session record shall contain:

- session identity, date, status, and domain;
- attendees and roles;
- objectives and review questions;
- admitted evidence and evidence-pack version;
- discussion record or attributable transcript;
- candidate and issued GF references;
- Founder questions and exact FD references, if any;
- dissent, alternatives, uncertainty, and deferred matters;
- affected Open Questions;
- actions that do not themselves authorise downstream work;
- recorder attestation;
- validation outcome;
- Founder acknowledgement or disposition where required.

## 12. Review Questions

Every session shall be driven by explicit Review Questions.

Each Review Question shall record:

- canonical identifier;
- question text;
- domain and session;
- decision purpose;
- source Open Question, if any;
- scope and exclusions;
- relevant evidence;
- expected output;
- decision need;
- status;
- disposition and rationale.

A Review Question does not imply that a Founder Decision is required. The session plan shall distinguish questions seeking evidence, findings, advice, or reserved Founder decisions.

## 13. Governance Finding Structure

### 13.1 Nature of a Governance Finding

A Governance Finding is an evidence-based review conclusion. It describes what the admitted evidence supports, does not support, or leaves uncertain.

A GF:

- is not a Founder Decision;
- is not a constitutional principle;
- is not a governance standard;
- cannot create authority;
- cannot close an Open Question by itself;
- must preserve contrary evidence and uncertainty.

### 13.2 Mandatory GF Fields

Every GF shall contain:

- canonical GF identifier;
- title;
- review and session identifiers;
- governance domain;
- related Review Question;
- exact finding statement;
- decision relevance;
- supporting Evidence Records;
- contrary or limiting evidence;
- analysis and rationale;
- confidence or uncertainty statement;
- scope and exclusions;
- affected Open Questions;
- related GFs;
- recommendation, if separately identified;
- validation status;
- lifecycle status;
- author, recorder, and dates.

### 13.3 GF Lifecycle

| State | Meaning | Permitted Transition Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate | Potential finding identified; not ready for reliance | Review Analyst |
| Draft | Structured finding with initial evidence links | Review Analyst |
| Evidence Validated | Evidence identity, admissibility, and links checked | Validator |
| Presented | Finding presented in an attributable review session | Review Coordinator |
| Dispositioned | Founder or authorised review process has accepted it for decision input, returned it, deferred it, or rejected reliance | Founder or expressly authorised review disposition |
| Superseded | Later attributable finding replaces it without deleting history | Founder or approved review change control |
| Archived | Retained after final review assembly | Evidence Custodian |

GF lifecycle status shall not be labelled `Approved`, `Active`, or `Constitutional`.

Returned or rejected findings shall remain in the audit trail with their disposition rationale.

## 14. Founder Decision Structure

### 14.1 Nature and Reserved Authority

A Founder Decision is an exact, attributable determination issued by the Founder in response to a stated decision question.

Only the Founder may:

- issue an FD;
- approve its exact authoritative wording;
- impose or remove conditions;
- accept risk;
- defer the decision;
- supersede or withdraw a prior FD.

The coding agent, analyst, recorder, coordinator, or validator may prepare decision questions and options but may not select an option or infer a decision.

### 14.2 Mandatory FD Fields

Every FD shall contain:

- canonical FD identifier;
- title and decision question;
- review and session identifiers;
- exact Founder-selected option or directive;
- exact decision wording;
- decision date;
- authority source and attributable record;
- related GFs and Evidence Records;
- rationale or explicit statement that no rationale was supplied;
- scope and exclusions;
- conditions;
- accepted risks, if any;
- deferred matters;
- affected Open Questions;
- affected documents and programme dependencies;
- effective treatment;
- supersession relationships;
- recorder and validation status.

No effective date, activation, constitutional status, or standard approval may be inferred when the Founder has not supplied it.

### 14.3 FD Lifecycle

| State | Meaning | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Decision Question Draft | Question and options prepared; no decision | Review Coordinator |
| Ready for Founder | Evidence and finding inputs validated for decision | Validator |
| Issued | Founder has made an attributable decision | Founder |
| Recorded | Exact decision and metadata preserved | Session Recorder |
| Decision Record Validated | Wording, source, relationships, and boundaries verified | Validator |
| Incorporated | Decision cited in an authorised downstream artefact | Downstream work package under its own authority |
| Superseded or Withdrawn | Founder expressly changes its treatment | Founder |
| Archived | Historical record preserved | Evidence Custodian |

`Issued` and `Recorded` do not automatically mean `Implemented`, `Constitutional`, `Active`, or `Effective`.

## 15. Open Question Handling

### 15.1 Existing Open Questions

The new review shall import the 23 FEF-WPK-001 Open Questions as unresolved inputs without changing their wording or status.

Each existing Open Question shall be mapped to:

- one or more review domains;
- a Review Question;
- planned session coverage;
- relevant evidence;
- related GFs;
- related FDs, if issued;
- final disposition;
- downstream document implications.

### 15.2 New Questions

New questions discovered during the review shall receive review-scoped Review Question identifiers. They shall not reuse or silently extend an existing Open Question identifier.

### 15.3 Permitted Dispositions

| Disposition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Open | Not yet resolved |
| Under Review | Admitted to a planned or current session |
| Answered by FD | An attributable Founder Decision answers the question |
| Deferred | Founder or approved review authority intentionally postpones it |
| Out of Review Scope | The Charter excludes it; downstream owner or route recorded |
| Superseded | An attributable decision replaces the question’s premise |
| Closed | Closure criteria and cited Founder authority are complete |

Only an attributable Founder Decision may resolve or close a governance Open Question. A GF, recommendation, validation result, or session completion is insufficient.

## 16. Constitutional Extraction Principles

### 16.1 Separation

The review may identify constitutional candidates. It shall not directly create, insert, approve, or activate constitutional text.

### 16.2 Candidate Tests

A proposition may be identified as a constitutional candidate only when it is:

1. supported by admitted evidence and a validated GF;
2. grounded in an attributable FD where Founder authority is required;
3. enduring rather than temporary or implementation-specific;
4. applicable at framework level rather than only to a single domain, document, or project;
5. concerned with authority, rights, obligations, boundaries, or fundamental governance philosophy;
6. stated at the minimum necessary level of abstraction;
7. compatible with recorded scope protections;
8. traceable to contrary evidence, uncertainty, and conditions;
9. separated from procedures, templates, tools, data models, and operational implementation;
10. designated as a candidate rather than constitutional law.

### 16.3 Extraction Record

Each candidate shall record:

- candidate identifier;
- proposition;
- supporting GFs and FDs;
- evidence chain;
- enduring-governance rationale;
- domain applicability;
- exclusions;
- conflicts and uncertainties;
- recommended destination;
- later consolidation authority required.

### 16.4 Separate Consolidation

Constitutional consolidation shall occur only through a separate authorised work package after the review is completed and approved. That work shall preserve one-to-one or many-to-one traceability and shall not silently merge, broaden, or reinterpret Founder Decisions.

## 17. Deferred Matters, Dissent, and Uncertainty

The review shall preserve:

- matters intentionally deferred;
- decisions not made;
- evidence gaps;
- conflicting sources;
- dissenting technical or governance views;
- conditions and expiry triggers;
- assumptions requiring later validation;
- questions outside the Charter.

Uncertainty shall not be resolved by drafting preference. A deferred matter shall record its authority, rationale, dependencies, and reconsideration trigger where supplied.

## 18. Review Validation

### 18.1 Validation Layers

| Layer | Required Validation |
|---|---|
| Evidence | Identity, provenance, integrity, admissibility, relevance, authority, limitations |
| Session | Entry gate, evidence-pack version, attendance, attributable record, outputs, unresolved matters |
| GF | Mandatory fields, evidence links, contrary evidence, uncertainty, scope, lifecycle state |
| FD | Exact Founder wording, authority source, related GF/evidence, conditions, non-effects, lifecycle state |
| Open Questions | Complete mapping, cited FD for resolution or closure, no silent status change |
| Constitutional Candidates | Candidate tests, traceability, abstraction, separate-consolidation boundary |
| Review Record | Counts, unique identifiers, links, status consistency, scope, completeness, preservation |

### 18.2 Validation Independence

The Validator shall not create missing evidence, choose a Founder option, or cure a substantive defect through inference. Any role combination affecting independence shall be disclosed in the validation report.

### 18.3 Validation Outcomes

Permitted outcomes are:

- Pass;
- Pass with recorded conditions;
- Return for correction;
- Incomplete;
- Blocked by named evidence or authority dependency.

A validation pass confirms record integrity only. It does not approve governance content or a standard.

## 19. Review Completion Criteria

The review may be presented for Founder completion approval only when:

1. the approved Charter and Review Plan are preserved;
2. every planned session is completed, cancelled, or deferred with attributable disposition;
3. every admitted source has an Evidence Record;
4. every Review Question has a recorded disposition;
5. every issued GF is evidence validated and lifecycle-dispositioned;
6. every issued FD is recorded exactly and validated;
7. every existing Open Question is mapped and its status is explicit;
8. new questions and deferred matters are registered;
9. constitutional candidates are separated from approved constitutional content;
10. session and cross-review traceability is complete;
11. conflicting evidence, dissent, conditions, and uncertainty are preserved;
12. all identifiers are unique and links resolve;
13. the final review validation report passes or records Founder-accepted conditions;
14. the Founder completes an attributable final review disposition.

Review completion does not:

- approve or amend the Constitution;
- approve or amend FEF-RGS-000;
- authorise FEF-WPK-001C, FEF-WPK-001D, or Engineering Discovery automatically;
- convert every GF into an FD;
- close every Open Question unless the required decisions exist.

## 20. Founder Approval Process

### 20.1 Charter Approval

Before the review begins, the Founder shall disposition this Charter as one of:

- Approved;
- Approved with conditions;
- Returned for amendment;
- Rejected;
- Deferred.

The Charter approval record shall contain:

- exact disposition;
- approved version;
- conditions and amendments;
- approval date;
- review identifier treatment;
- commencement authority;
- effective treatment;
- next authorised action.

No coding agent or reviewer may complete Founder-only approval fields.

### 20.2 Session Decisions

During the review:

- decision questions shall be presented with evidence and GF traceability;
- the Founder may decide, condition, defer, return, or decline a decision;
- exact wording shall be confirmed before an FD is treated as issued;
- session acknowledgement shall not be treated as final review approval.

### 20.3 Final Review Approval

After completion validation, the Founder shall disposition the final review record as one of:

- Approved as the attributable governance baseline;
- Approved with conditions;
- Returned for further review;
- Partially approved with explicit exclusions;
- Rejected;
- Deferred.

The final approval record shall identify:

- approved review version and record set;
- included GFs and their final review dispositions;
- issued FDs and any exclusions;
- accepted conditions or risks;
- unresolved and deferred matters;
- Open Question dispositions;
- constitutional candidates authorised for later consolidation;
- downstream work authorised, if any;
- non-effects.

Final review approval makes the approved record an authoritative source within its stated scope. It does not itself amend the Constitution or FEF-RGS-000 unless the Founder separately and expressly grants that effect through an applicable authority process.

## 21. Charter Change Control

### 21.1 Before Approval

Draft changes shall increment the draft version and be recorded in the Charter validation or review package.

### 21.2 After Approval

An approved Charter may be amended only through:

1. a stated amendment purpose;
2. impact assessment;
3. preserved redline or change record;
4. validation;
5. Founder approval;
6. new version assignment;
7. explicit treatment of sessions already conducted.

No amendment may retroactively change the meaning of evidence, GFs, FDs, or session records. Earlier Charter versions shall be preserved.

### 21.3 Exceptions

Any exception shall identify:

- the Charter clause;
- reason;
- scope;
- duration;
- risks;
- compensating controls;
- approval authority;
- expiry or review trigger.

An exception cannot delegate Founder decision authority or permit evidence reconstruction.

## 23. Preservation and Auditability

Review records shall be:

- created contemporaneously;
- stored in controlled repository locations;
- versioned without overwriting history;
- linked using stable relative references;
- integrity-fingerprinted at defined checkpoints;
- attributable to their recorder and authority;
- retained with rejected, returned, superseded, and deferred records;
- protected from silent editing after Founder disposition.

If a source cannot be preserved in the repository, its Evidence Record shall preserve location, access route, integrity metadata, custodian, and any lawful access restriction.
