### 1.1 Purpose

This draft proposes the governance principles under which research activities conducted within the Founder Engineering Framework (FEF) should be commissioned, designed, performed, validated, reported, preserved, and governed.

The proposed standard is intended to make research traceable, evidence-based, reproducible where practicable, explicit about uncertainty, and suitable for governed decision-making.

### 1.2 Status and Authority Boundary

This document prepares a draft standard only. It does not approve research governance, create constitutional authority, bind a Founder project, or authorise research execution.

Every requirement in this draft remains a recommendation until the Founder approves the standard. No project has adopted this draft.

Within this draft:

- **shall** denotes a proposed mandatory control if the standard is approved;
- **should** denotes a proposed recommendation;
- **may** denotes a proposed permission;
- **must not** denotes a proposed prohibition if the standard is approved.

These terms express drafting intent, not current authority.

### 1.3 Scope

This draft addresses governance common to FEF research, including:

- research purpose, authority, and accountability;
- governance principles and operational research principles;
- governance chain, classification, and research types;
- research design and lifecycle control;
- evidence classification and provenance;
- research artefacts, quality, and validation;
- preservation and research-standard governance.

### 1.4 Explicit Exclusions

This draft does not:

- define the operating procedure for discovering, extracting, or classifying engineering assets;
- prescribe project-specific search locations or extraction tools;
- define the recommendation method for incorporating discovered assets into FEF;
- approve FEF-RDS-001;
- establish constitutional clauses or final authority levels;
- initiate the Engineering Asset Discovery Programme;
- override project-specific governance, access controls, or ownership.

Those operational discovery matters belong to FEF-RDS-001 or later approved instruments.

## 2. Research Philosophy

FEF research should convert available evidence into traceable findings and clearly bounded recommendations without disguising uncertainty as fact.

Research is a governed engineering activity, not an informal collection exercise. Its value depends on the integrity of its question, evidence, method, analysis, validation, and preserved record.

The proposed philosophy is:

1. research exists to support informed decisions, not to manufacture support for a predetermined conclusion;
2. evidence should remain distinguishable from interpretation and recommendation;
3. provenance should be preserved so that a reviewer can understand where a claim came from and how it changed;
4. uncertainty, absence of evidence, disagreement, and negative findings are legitimate research results;
5. research depth should be proportionate to decision impact, risk, reversibility, and available evidence;
6. project authority and access restrictions remain in force during FEF research;
7. AI systems may assist research but may not approve evidence, findings, standards, or decisions;
8. research records should allow later reviewers to reconstruct material reasoning.

## 3. Research Governance Principles

The following principles are intended to govern the research system as enduring constraints. They are distinct from the operational conduct principles in Section 5.

### 3.1 Evidence First

Governance decisions supported by research should be grounded in identifiable evidence. Preference, seniority, or confidence must not be represented as evidentiary proof.

### 3.2 Objectivity

Research governance should require fair evaluation of supporting, contrary, and inconclusive evidence. Research must not be structured to guarantee a predetermined outcome.

### 3.3 Independence

Research governance should provide separation proportionate to the impact and reversibility of the decision. The required degree of independence between commissioning, execution, validation, review, and decision remains subject to Founder decision.

### 3.4 Traceability

Material claims, findings, recommendations, reviews, and decisions should be traceable to their evidence, analysis, responsible roles, and change history.

### 3.5 Transparency

Research governance should make scope, method, assumptions, uncertainty, conflicts, limitations, and review status visible to authorised reviewers.

Transparency does not override security, privacy, confidentiality, ownership, or access restrictions.

### 3.6 Accountability

Every governed research responsibility should have an identified accountable role, a defined authority source, and a preserved record of material actions and dispositions.

### 3.7 Reproducibility and Reconstruction

Research governance should require enough method, provenance, and analysis detail for a competent reviewer to reproduce results where practicable or reconstruct the material reasoning where exact reproduction is not possible.

### 3.8 Preservation of Uncertainty

Uncertainty must remain visible until evidence or an authorised decision resolves it. Research governance must not permit assumptions, estimates, or unresolved conflicts to be silently converted into facts.

### 3.9 Proportional Governance

The depth of governance, separation, evidence, validation, and preservation should be proportionate to risk, consequence, novelty, sensitivity, and reversibility.

### 3.10 Authority Integrity

Research may inform authority but must not manufacture it. A research finding, recommendation, technical review, or validation outcome does not become a Founder decision unless an authorised decision record says so.

## 4. Definitions

**Assumption:** A proposition temporarily accepted to permit work to proceed, without being represented as verified fact.

**Claim:** A statement presented for evaluation and supported, contradicted, or left unresolved by evidence.

**Commissioning authority:** The person or governed body authorised to initiate a research activity and define its decision purpose. The appointment and limits of this role remain an open governance question.

**Conflict of evidence:** A condition in which credible evidence sources support materially incompatible interpretations.

**Derived evidence:** A transformation, analysis, summary, measurement, or extraction produced from one or more source artefacts.

**Evidence:** Information or an artefact used to support, challenge, qualify, or contextualise a claim.

**Evidence record:** A controlled artefact that identifies evidence, its origin, custody, state, transformations, and relationship to research claims.

**Finding:** A research conclusion supported by identified evidence and stated with its applicable scope, confidence, and limitations.

**Findings record:** A controlled artefact containing findings and their traceability to research questions, evidence, analysis, confidence, limitations, and counterevidence.

**Governance responsibility:** A responsibility for authorising, constraining, reviewing, approving, accepting risk, or overseeing research rather than performing the research analysis itself.

**Primary evidence:** An original or authoritative artefact created by, or directly recording, the system, event, decision, or practice under study.

**Provenance:** The documented origin, custody, history, transformations, and authenticity assessment of evidence.

**Recommendation:** A proposed course of action derived from findings. A recommendation is not an approval or decision.

**Recommendation record:** A controlled artefact that relates proposed actions to findings, benefits, risks, dependencies, alternatives, reversibility, and required decision authority.

**Research activity:** A governed body of execution work that investigates defined questions through planned evidence collection, analysis, validation, and reporting.

**Research classification:** A purpose-based category that describes what a research activity is intended to accomplish.

**Research design:** The controlled pre-execution definition of decision purpose, questions, scope, method, evidence, deliverables, validation, and success criteria.

**Research execution responsibility:** A responsibility for designing, collecting, assessing, analysing, synthesising, validating, reporting, or preserving research within granted authority.

**Research lead:** The person accountable for managing a research activity within the authority granted by the commissioning authority.

**Research plan:** The controlled description of questions, scope, method, evidence needs, roles, risks, deliverables, and validation approach for a research activity.

**Research programme:** A governed collection of related research activities coordinated to support a defined programme purpose or decision set.

**Research record:** The preserved body of plans, evidence metadata, analyses, findings, reviews, decisions, and closure information associated with a research activity.

**Research register:** A controlled index of research activities or artefacts, their identifiers, ownership, status, relationships, and locations.

**Research report:** A controlled communication of research purpose, method, evidence basis, analysis, findings, limitations, and recommendations.

**Research standard:** A governed document that specifies requirements or methods for FEF research.

**Research type:** A domain-based category describing the subject area in which research is conducted.

**Source artefact:** The original file, repository object, record, testimony, observation, or other item from which evidence is obtained.

**Technical reviewer:** A reviewer who assesses methodological soundness, technical accuracy, traceability, and scope compliance without approving governance.

**Uncertainty:** A known limitation in evidence, method, interpretation, completeness, or confidence.

**Validation:** A planned assessment of whether research methods, evidence, analysis, and outputs are sufficiently reliable for their stated purpose.

**Validator:** A reviewer assigned to perform validation with an appropriate degree of independence. Required independence criteria remain unresolved.

## 5. Operational Research Principles

If approved, FEF research activities shall apply the following proposed conduct principles. These operational principles implement, but do not replace, the governance principles in Section 3.

### 5.1 Purpose Before Collection

Research shall begin with a stated decision purpose and defined research questions. Evidence collection shall not begin solely because information is available.

### 5.2 Authority Before Action

The commissioning basis, permitted scope, access authority, and prohibited actions shall be recorded before research execution.

### 5.3 Evidence Before Conclusion

Material findings shall cite identifiable evidence. Unsupported statements shall be labelled as assumptions, hypotheses, or open questions.

### 5.4 Provenance by Default

Evidence shall receive sufficient provenance metadata to allow a reviewer to identify its source, state, custody, and transformations.

### 5.5 Separation of Record Types

Research outputs shall distinguish:

- observed facts;
- source assertions;
- analyst interpretations;
- assumptions;
- findings;
- recommendations;
- approvals and decisions.

### 5.6 Uncertainty Must Remain Visible

Researchers shall record material uncertainty, gaps, conflicts, inaccessible sources, and limitations. They must not close a governance question through convenience or unstated assumption.

### 5.7 Proportionality

Research controls should be proportionate to the consequence, reversibility, novelty, sensitivity, and evidentiary difficulty of the decision being supported.

### 5.8 Reproducibility and Reconstruction

Research shall preserve enough method and traceability information for a competent reviewer to reconstruct material steps. Exact reproduction may be limited by access, confidentiality, changing systems, or ephemeral evidence; any limitation shall be recorded.

### 5.9 Respect for Source Authority

Research shall not alter the authority, ownership, confidentiality, or governance of a source project. Extraction into an FEF research record does not transfer authority or ownership.

### 5.10 Controlled Use of AI

AI-generated summaries, classifications, or analyses shall be identified as derived outputs, linked to their source evidence, and subjected to human review appropriate to their impact. AI systems must not be recorded as approval authorities.

### 5.11 Preservation of Contrary Evidence

Material evidence that contradicts a preferred finding shall be retained, analysed, and disclosed.

### 5.12 Scope Discipline

Researchers shall not expand a research activity beyond its authorised questions and boundaries without recording and obtaining the required scope decision.

## 6. Governance Chain

### 6.1 Conceptual Relationship

The proposed governance relationship is:

```text
Founder
   ↓
Research Governance Standard
   ↓
Research Standards
   ↓
Research Programmes
   ↓
Research Activities
   ↓
Research Records
   ↓
Founder Decisions
```

### 6.2 Interpretation

This model is conceptual, not implementation-specific. It illustrates the intended flow from authority and governing controls, through research execution and preserved evidence, back to Founder decision-making.

The diagram does not:

- declare the Founder’s future constitutional powers;
- make this draft standard authoritative;
- imply that every research result requires a Founder decision;
- assign ownership of every intermediate artefact;
- replace project-specific authority or access governance;
- authorise any current programme or activity.

### 6.3 Relationship Rules

If approved:

1. research standards shall conform to the approved Research Governance Standard;
2. research programmes shall identify the standards governing their activities;
3. research activities shall operate within programme and standard boundaries;
4. research records shall preserve evidence, reasoning, review, and decision traceability;
5. Founder decisions shall remain distinguishable from the research that informs them.

The detailed transition authorities and decision points within this chain remain unresolved.

## 7. Research Classification

### 7.1 Purpose

Research classification describes the primary purpose of a research activity. It classifies research work, not engineering assets, documents, source projects, or authority levels.

### 7.2 Proposed Classifications

**Discovery research:** Identifies what evidence, practices, assets, conditions, or gaps exist.

**Validation research:** Tests whether a claim, implementation, control, method, or prior finding is sufficiently supported.

**Comparative research:** Evaluates alternatives, patterns, or sources against declared comparison criteria.

**Governance research:** Investigates authority, accountability, decision, policy, or control questions.

**Compliance research:** Assesses conformance to identified obligations, standards, policies, or controls.

**Framework evolution research:** Evaluates evidence relevant to creating, revising, or retiring FEF content.

**Audit research:** Independently examines records and evidence against defined audit criteria.

**Historical research:** Reconstructs prior decisions, implementations, events, or practice evolution from preserved evidence.

### 7.3 Classification Rules

A research plan should identify one primary classification and may identify secondary classifications where purposes overlap.

Classification shall not determine evidentiary quality, approval authority, confidentiality, or research outcome. Selection rules, combinations, and any classification-specific controls remain open questions.

## 8. Research Types

### 8.1 Purpose

Research type describes the subject domain of an activity. Type and classification answer different questions:

- **classification:** Why is the research being performed?
- **type:** What domain is being researched?

### 8.2 Proposed Types

**Governance research:** Examines governance models, authority relationships, approvals, controls, and institutional responsibilities.

**Engineering research:** Examines engineering methods, implementations, systems, tooling, quality, reliability, security, and technical practices.

**Portfolio research:** Examines patterns, differences, risks, and reusable knowledge across Founder projects.

**Architecture research:** Examines system, programme, information, documentation, or framework architecture.

**Compliance research:** Examines legal, regulatory, contractual, policy, security, privacy, or standards obligations.

**Knowledge preservation research:** Examines records, provenance, retention, reconstruction, migration, and institutional memory.

### 8.3 Non-Restrictive Taxonomy

The taxonomy supports consistent planning and future method standards without constraining legitimate research. A research activity may span multiple types.

Adding, combining, or specialising types shall not silently change authority, scope, or required controls. Formal type-selection and extension rules remain unresolved.

## 9. Research Authority Model

### 9.1 Status of the Model

The following model is recommended for review. It does not assign constitutional authority or confirm permanent roles. Role appointment, delegation, separation, and escalation rules remain open questions.

### 9.2 Governance Responsibilities

Governance responsibilities determine whether research may proceed, under what constraints, how it is reviewed, and who may make or record decisions. Proposed governance responsibilities include:

- establishing or approving applicable research standards;
- commissioning research and defining its decision purpose;
- approving scope, access, risk, and material changes;
- setting validation and review expectations;
- accepting or rejecting conditions, exceptions, or residual risk;
- approving, rejecting, or returning standards and Founder decision proposals;
- maintaining oversight without directing a predetermined research outcome.

Governance responsibility does not by itself authorise a role to collect evidence, perform analysis, validate its own work, or alter research records.

### 9.3 Research Execution Responsibilities

Execution responsibilities perform the research within granted authority. Proposed execution responsibilities include:

- designing research;
- acquiring and registering evidence;
- assessing provenance and quality;
- analysing and synthesising evidence;
- preparing findings and recommendations;
- validating methods and outputs;
- reporting, closing, and preserving research records.

Execution responsibility does not authorise approval of governance, a standard, a programme, or a Founder decision.

### 9.4 Proposed Roles and Responsibility Separation

**Founder — governance responsibility:** Reviews and, where required by FEF governance, approves, rejects, or returns research standards and Founder decisions. The Founder’s approval of the Phase 0 architecture does not constitute approval of this draft.

**Commissioning authority — governance responsibility:** Authorises a defined research activity, its purpose, boundaries, resources, and intended decision use within authority granted elsewhere.

**Research sponsor — governance-support responsibility:** Maintains the decision need, access support, and organisational context. Whether this role is distinct from the commissioning authority remains unresolved.

**Research lead — execution responsibility:** Prepares the research design and plan, assigns work, maintains controls, ensures traceability, and submits deliverables for validation and review. The research lead does not approve governance.

**Researcher — execution responsibility:** Collects and analyses evidence within the authorised plan, records provenance, distinguishes fact from inference, and discloses limitations and conflicts.

**Technical reviewer — review responsibility:** Assesses technical coherence, method, evidence use, traceability, architecture, and scope compliance. Technical Review does not constitute Founder approval.

**Validator — validation responsibility:** Executes the validation plan and records pass, fail, conditional, or inconclusive results without rewriting the research conclusion or approving governance.

**Record custodian — preservation responsibility:** Preserves the controlled research record, access conditions, identifiers, versions, relationships, and change history without changing substantive findings.

### 9.5 Proposed Separation of Powers

For research supporting high-impact or difficult-to-reverse decisions, the commissioning authority, researcher, validator, and decision authority should not all be the same person.

Any permitted role combination, minimum independence requirement, or exception mechanism requires Founder decision and is not resolved by this draft.

### 9.6 Authority Record

Each research activity shall record, at minimum:

- the commissioning authority and source of authority;
- the research lead;
- assigned researchers and reviewers;
- the validator;
- the intended decision owner;
- governance and execution responsibilities;
- access constraints;
- approval checkpoints;
- unresolved role conflicts.

## 10. Research Artefacts

### 10.1 Purpose

The following proposed artefacts establish common terminology for future research standards. They describe distinct information responsibilities; they do not require separate files where a controlled combined document preserves the distinctions and traceability.

### 10.2 Principal Artefacts

**Research record:** The complete preserved body of controlled information for a research activity, including design, evidence metadata, analysis, findings, recommendations, reviews, validation, decisions, and closure.

**Research register:** A controlled index used to locate and govern research activities or artefacts. It should record identifiers, title, ownership, type, classification, status, relationships, and controlled location.

**Research report:** A structured communication of decision purpose, questions, scope, method, evidence basis, analysis, findings, uncertainty, limitations, and recommendations.

**Evidence record:** A controlled description of an evidence item, including its identifier, source, provenance, custody, transformations, assessment, limitations, and research relationships.

**Findings record:** A controlled set of findings linked to research questions, evidence, analysis, confidence, limitations, conflicts, and validation status.

**Recommendation record:** A controlled set of proposed actions linked to findings, expected benefits, risks, dependencies, alternatives, reversibility, and required decision authority.

### 10.3 Artefact Relationships

If approved:

1. the research register shall identify the research activity and controlled locations;
2. evidence records shall identify the evidence used by the activity;
3. findings records shall trace conclusions to evidence and analysis;
4. recommendation records shall trace proposed actions to findings;
5. the research report shall communicate the material research outcome;
6. the research record shall preserve these artefacts and their review history.

### 10.4 Artefact Control

Every controlled artefact should identify its status, version, responsible role, related research activity, and review state. Whether each artefact must be a separate document, and which artefacts are mandatory for each research classification, remain open questions.

Section 17 defines the broader proposed deliverable set. This section defines shared artefact meanings.

## 11. Research Design Rules

### 11.1 Pre-Execution Requirement

Research execution shall not begin until a proportionate research design is recorded and the required commissioning and access authority is identified.

Urgency may affect depth but must not silently remove authority, scope, provenance, or uncertainty controls. Any emergency or expedited design mechanism requires approval and remains unresolved.

### 11.2 Minimum Design Elements

**Decision purpose:** Identify the decision, governance need, or knowledge need that the research is intended to support. Research does not itself make the decision.

**Research questions:** State answerable questions and, where useful, sub-questions. Each question should later map to a finding, non-finding, or unresolved result.

**Scope:** Define included subjects, systems, projects, time periods, repositories, stakeholders, and evidence boundaries.

**Non-scope:** State material exclusions and adjacent matters that the research will not resolve.

**Research classification and type:** Identify the primary purpose classification from Section 7 and applicable subject types from Section 8.

**Evidence sources:** Identify expected source classes, locations, access constraints, known gaps, and provenance needs without presuming that expected evidence exists.

**Method:** Identify how evidence will be selected, sampled, acquired, assessed, analysed, synthesised, and preserved. Detailed asset-discovery methods belong to FEF-RDS-001 where applicable.

**Expected deliverables:** Identify the artefacts and deliverables required from Sections 10 and 17, including any proportionate tailoring.

**Validation method:** Define validation criteria, independence, sampling, traceability tests, outcomes, remediation, and revalidation triggers.

**Success criteria:** Define what completion means for the research activity. Success criteria must allow a legitimate inconclusive or negative finding where evidence does not support a conclusion.

### 11.3 Additional Design Controls

The design should also record:

- authority source and assigned roles;
- assumptions and open questions;
- risks, sensitivity, confidentiality, and access controls;
- dependencies and constraints;
- quality criteria;
- change-control method;
- closure and preservation conditions.

### 11.4 Design Review

The research design should be reviewed for decision relevance, feasibility, authority, scope, method fitness, evidence availability, bias risk, validation adequacy, and deliverable clarity.

Who approves a research design, which changes require reapproval, and what minimum review applies to each classification remain open governance questions.

## 12. Research Lifecycle

The proposed research lifecycle contains nine controlled stages. Tailoring may be permitted only if the rationale and omitted controls are recorded.

This lifecycle governs research activities. It is distinct from the Research Standard Lifecycle in Section 18.

### 12.1 Commission

Define the decision purpose, commissioning basis, intended users, urgency, constraints, and expected value.

**Minimum output:** research commission or charter.

### 12.2 Design and Plan

Apply Section 11 to define questions, scope, exclusions, classification, type, method, evidence needs, roles, risks, quality criteria, validation approach, deliverables, and closure conditions.

**Minimum output:** reviewed research design and plan.

### 12.3 Acquire

Obtain evidence within authorised access boundaries. Register source artefacts and preserve provenance at the point of acquisition.

**Minimum output:** evidence and provenance records.

The detailed discovery and extraction procedure for engineering assets belongs to FEF-RDS-001 and is not specified here.

### 12.4 Assess

Assess authenticity, relevance, completeness, currency, authority context, sensitivity, and limitations. Quarantine or label evidence whose integrity cannot be established.

**Minimum output:** evidence assessment status and identified gaps.

### 12.5 Analyse

Evaluate evidence against the research questions using the planned method. Record interpretations, competing explanations, conflicts, and uncertainty.

**Minimum output:** traceable analysis record.

### 12.6 Synthesise

Produce findings and recommendations while preserving the distinction between evidence, interpretation, and proposed action.

**Minimum output:** draft findings and recommendations.

### 12.7 Validate

Execute the validation plan. Test traceability, method application, evidence sufficiency, internal consistency, limitations, and material counterevidence.

**Minimum output:** validation report.

### 12.8 Review and Decision Support

Submit the research deliverables to the required technical and governance reviews. Record reviewer findings and disposition without representing review as approval.

**Minimum output:** review record and unresolved findings.

### 12.9 Close and Preserve

Record completion status, unresolved matters, decision handoff, retained evidence, access conditions, lessons, and required follow-up.

**Minimum output:** closure and preservation record.

## 13. Evidence Hierarchy

### 13.1 Proposed Evidence Classes

The following hierarchy is recommended as a starting model:

1. **Primary controlled artefacts:** original constitutions, approved decisions, source code at an identified revision, executed records, system-generated records, and other controlled originals.
2. **Contemporaneous supporting records:** issue histories, review records, meeting records, test results, operational logs, and communications created near the relevant event.
3. **Validated derived evidence:** verified extracts, analyses, inventories, measurements, and summaries with traceable transformations.
4. **Contextual testimony and recollection:** interviews, explanations, and retrospective accounts whose source and date are recorded.
5. **Unverified assertions and generated material:** claims without adequate supporting evidence, including unvalidated AI-generated content.

### 13.2 Application

Evidence class shall inform, but shall not alone determine, evidentiary weight. Evaluation shall also consider authenticity, relevance, authority context, currency, completeness, independence, and consistency with other evidence.

An approved primary artefact may establish what was authorised but may not prove what was implemented. A system record may establish implementation behaviour but may not establish governance approval.

### 13.3 Conflict Handling

When evidence conflicts, researchers shall:

1. preserve each material source;
2. identify the nature of the conflict;
3. assess provenance and context;
4. seek corroborating or disconfirming evidence;
5. state the unresolved conflict and its effect on confidence;
6. avoid selecting a preferred source without recorded rationale.

### 13.4 Absence of Evidence

The absence of located evidence shall not automatically be treated as evidence that an event, decision, or practice did not exist. The search scope, access limitations, and expected evidence shall be recorded.

## 14. Provenance Standard

### 14.1 Minimum Proposed Metadata

Each material evidence item shall have a stable evidence identifier and, where applicable:

- title or concise description;
- source project or context;
- source type;
- repository, system, collection, or custodian;
- precise locator, path, record key, or reference;
- author or originator, if known;
- creation date, if known;
- acquisition date;
- acquiring researcher;
- version, revision, commit, checksum, or immutable identifier when practicable;
- original or derived status;
- parent evidence identifiers for derived evidence;
- transformation or extraction history;
- access and confidentiality restrictions;
- authenticity assessment;
- review and validation status;
- known gaps, corruption, or limitations;
- retention location.

Unknown values shall be marked unknown; they must not be invented.

### 14.2 Transformation Traceability

A derived evidence item shall identify its source items and material transformations. Summarisation, translation, filtering, sampling, normalisation, redaction, parsing, and AI-assisted processing shall be recorded when they may affect interpretation.

### 14.3 Chain of Custody

Where evidence integrity or sensitivity is material, the record should identify custody transfers, copies, modifications, access events, and integrity checks. The threshold requiring formal chain-of-custody control remains an open question.

### 14.4 Stable Referencing

Research claims shall cite evidence identifiers rather than relying only on mutable filenames, conversational references, or unstored links.

### 14.5 Source Preservation

Source artefacts shall not be modified to make them conform to a research conclusion. Working copies and redacted copies shall be identified as such and linked to the preserved source where access permits.

## 15. Research Quality

### 15.1 Quality Attributes

Research quality shall be assessed against:

- **purpose fitness:** answers the authorised questions at the required decision depth;
- **traceability:** material claims connect to evidence and analysis;
- **method integrity:** the recorded method is appropriate and applied consistently;
- **provenance completeness:** evidence origin and transformation are visible;
- **scope compliance:** work remains within authorised boundaries;
- **balance:** material supporting and contrary evidence are addressed;
- **uncertainty transparency:** gaps and confidence limits are explicit;
- **reconstructability:** a competent reviewer can follow material reasoning;
- **clarity:** facts, interpretations, recommendations, and decisions are distinguishable;
- **preservation:** the research record remains accessible under applicable controls.

### 15.2 Quality Planning

The research design shall define quality criteria and evidence of completion before analysis begins. Numeric thresholds, sampling rules, and mandatory review levels should be defined by the applicable method standard or research design; this draft does not invent universal thresholds.

### 15.3 Quality Findings

Quality findings shall be classified as:

- **critical:** undermines authority, integrity, legality, safety, or the ability to rely on the research;
- **major:** materially weakens a finding or prevents a required lifecycle control;
- **minor:** reduces clarity or completeness without invalidating a material finding;
- **observation:** improvement opportunity or risk not constituting non-conformance.

Use of these classes as formal release gates requires approval.

### 15.4 Research Misconduct and Error

Fabrication, concealed provenance, selective removal of material counterevidence, invented approval, and unrecorded material transformation are incompatible with this proposed standard.

Suspected misconduct, material error, or compromised evidence shall be preserved and escalated through an approved mechanism. The escalation mechanism remains unresolved.

## 16. Validation

### 16.1 Validation Purpose

Validation shall determine whether the research record is sufficiently reliable and complete for its stated purpose. Validation does not convert recommendations into decisions or approve a standard.

### 16.2 Validation Plan

The validation plan shall identify:

- validation scope and criteria;
- validator and required independence;
- evidence and claims to sample or inspect;
- methods for testing traceability and reproducibility;
- treatment of exceptions and inconclusive results;
- required remediation and revalidation;
- reporting and escalation path.

### 16.3 Minimum Validation Tests

Validation should test:

1. authority and scope are recorded;
2. the research design contains the minimum elements in Section 11;
3. questions map to findings or explicit non-findings;
4. material claims map to evidence;
5. evidence carries required provenance;
6. transformations are traceable;
7. assumptions and uncertainty are disclosed;
8. contradictory evidence is addressed;
9. methods match the research design;
10. recommendations remain distinct from approvals;
11. artefacts, deliverables, and preservation records are complete.

### 16.4 Validation Outcomes

A validation report should record one of:

- **Pass:** stated criteria are satisfied;
- **Pass with conditions:** use is limited by recorded conditions;
- **Fail:** material criteria are not satisfied;
- **Inconclusive:** available evidence cannot support a validation conclusion.

The authority to accept conditions, risk, or inconclusive research remains unresolved.

### 16.5 Revalidation

Material changes to evidence, method, findings, scope, intended decision use, or research design shall trigger an assessment of whether revalidation is required.

## 17. Research Deliverables

### 17.1 Proposed Core Deliverables

A governed research activity should produce, as proportionate to its impact:

1. research commission or charter;
2. research design and plan;
3. research-register entry;
4. evidence and provenance records;
5. assumptions register;
6. open questions register;
7. analysis record;
8. findings record;
9. recommendation record;
10. research report;
11. decision log;
12. validation report;
13. review findings and dispositions;
14. closure and preservation record.

Which deliverables are universally mandatory and which may be tailored remains subject to Founder decision.

Section 10 defines the principal artefacts used within this deliverable set.

### 17.2 Deliverable Identity

Each controlled deliverable shall state:

- identifier and title;
- version;
- status;
- responsible preparer or role;
- review and approval state;
- related research activity;
- applicable standard and method;
- dependencies;
- confidentiality or access classification where required;
- change history.

### 17.3 Findings and Recommendations

Each material finding should state its question, conclusion, evidence references, analysis basis, scope, confidence, limitations, and counterevidence.

Each recommendation should state the finding on which it depends, expected benefit, material risks, dependencies, alternatives, reversibility, and required decision authority.

### 17.4 Preservation

Closure shall preserve controlled deliverables and sufficient supporting records to maintain traceability. Retention periods, archival formats, and deletion authority remain open governance questions.

## 18. Research Standard Lifecycle

### 18.1 Purpose and Boundary

This proposed lifecycle governs research standards themselves. It is distinct from the Research Lifecycle in Section 12, which governs research activities.

The lifecycle stages below define candidate terminology only. Including **Approved**, **Active**, **Superseded**, or **Archived** does not assign any of those states to FEF-RGS-000 or another standard.

### 18.2 Proposed Lifecycle Stages

**Draft:** Content is being prepared or amended. It is non-authoritative and may not be represented as adopted.

**Technical Review:** The draft is being assessed for architecture, method, consistency, traceability, feasibility, and authority-boundary compliance. Technical Review does not approve the standard.

**Founder Review:** The technically reviewed draft is presented for an explicit Founder decision. Entry into Founder Review does not imply approval.

**Approved:** The Founder has explicitly approved an identified version, with recorded evidence, conditions, and intended effective treatment. Whether approval and activation may be separate decisions remains unresolved.

**Active:** An approved version is effective for the scope and date stated in its activation record. No FEF research standard is declared Active by this draft.

**Superseded:** A previously approved version has been replaced for a defined scope by another approved version. Supersession does not erase historical applicability or evidence.

**Archived:** A version is retained as historical evidence and is not current governing authority. Preservation of the v0.1 draft in this repository is a traceability action and does not itself apply this proposed formal lifecycle state.

### 18.3 Proposed Transition Controls

Every lifecycle transition should record:

- standard identifier and exact version;
- source and scope of transition authority;
- prior and new state;
- decision date and effective date where applicable;
- conditions, limitations, and affected scope;
- unresolved findings and accepted risks;
- supersession or migration treatment;
- responsible custodian;
- preserved evidence.

### 18.4 Unresolved Lifecycle Governance

The following matters require Founder decision:

- transition authority for each stage;
- required entry and exit criteria;
- whether Approved and Active are separate states;
- effective-date and retrospective-application rules;
- emergency, conditional, suspension, withdrawal, and rejection states;
- supersession and migration controls;
- archival and retention authority.

## 19. Governance of Research Standards

### 19.1 Approval Boundary

Every FEF research standard is subject to Founder review and approval before it becomes authoritative. Technical Review may recommend readiness but does not constitute Founder approval.

FEF-RGS-000 v0.2 remains a non-authoritative draft until an explicit Founder decision records approval, approved version, effective treatment, and any conditions.

### 19.2 Proposed Change Lifecycle

A change to a research standard should include:

1. a controlled change proposal;
2. the problem, evidence, and intended outcome;
3. impact on active and completed research;
4. compatibility and migration considerations;
5. technical review;
6. validation against the standard’s purpose and authority boundary;
7. Founder decision;
8. lifecycle transition, versioning, communication, and preservation.

Section 18 defines the proposed lifecycle terminology for those transitions.

### 19.3 Versioning

Draft versions shall remain distinguishable from approved releases. An approved versioning scheme, release-state model, and effective-date policy have not yet been established.

### 19.4 Relationship to FEF-RDS-001

FEF-RGS-000 is intended to govern research principles and controls. FEF-RDS-001 is intended to define the method for engineering asset discovery, extraction, and framework recommendation.

FEF-RDS-001 shall not be merged into this draft. When prepared, it should:

- conform to the approved version of FEF-RGS-000;
- define its operational method without changing research governance;
- identify any required exception or unresolved conflict;
- retain traceability to the evidence and decisions that justify its controls.

### 19.5 Exceptions and Deviations

No research-standard exception or deviation mechanism is approved. A future mechanism should identify request authority, rationale, risk, duration, compensating controls, approval, review, expiry, and preserved record.

### 19.6 Periodic Review

The review cadence, review triggers, responsible custodian, and withdrawal process for research standards remain open questions.

### 19.7 Draft Disposition

This draft is suitable for Founder Review only after the FEF-WPK-001A validation report records successful structural, traceability, authority-boundary, scope-separation, supporting-artefact, and cross-reference checks.

Founder Review may approve, reject, return, or otherwise direct the draft through an explicit decision. Neither completion of Technical Review amendments nor successful validation constitutes Founder approval.
