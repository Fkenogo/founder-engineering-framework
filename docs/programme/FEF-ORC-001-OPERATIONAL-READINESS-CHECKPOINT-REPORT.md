## 1. Purpose

ORC-001 concludes the programme-level readiness review following D1 — Governance Authority and determines whether the Founder Engineering Framework can sustain Operational Mode before D2 begins.

The checkpoint evaluates the Framework and its execution controls. It is not another governance-domain review, does not reopen D1, and does not itself authorise D2.

The current Framework operating mode was recorded as **Operational** by FEF-FGR-002-FD-009. ORC-001 tests whether that transition is sufficiently evidenced and controlled for sustained reuse across the remaining review domains.

## 6. Material Lessons Learned

1. **The operational lifecycle is sufficiently mature for reuse.** D1 completed every material gate from mobilisation through closure without collapsing evidence, analysis, validation, or decision authority.
2. **Authority boundaries worked.** The Founder remained the sole disposition and decision authority; operational roles remained bounded even when combined in one agent.
3. **Validation must remain mandatory.** Mechanical validation caught state, count, link, and wording issues. The disclosed non-independent condition is a limitation, not a reason to remove validation.
4. **Founder review preparation can be standardised.** A consolidated package is useful; separate finding briefs, sequencing reports, agendas, and summaries should be produced only when they reduce a specific review risk.
5. **Narrative duplication occurred.** Exact finding content, non-effects, readiness, and dependencies were repeated across multiple D1 artefacts. Future domains should cite canonical records instead of restating them.
6. **First-use artefact volume was proportionate to framework proving, but should not be copied automatically.** D1 needed to demonstrate the lifecycle. Future artefacts must pass the necessity gate.
7. **Synchronisation and traceability checks are repeatable.** Register counts, lifecycle states, link resolution, identifier uniqueness, and protected-file comparisons are deterministic loop candidates.
8. **Closure can use a governed loop.** The closure pattern is stable when inputs are validated decisions, reconciled registers, protected-state checks, and a concise closure report.

## 7. Bounded Simplifications Before D2

### 7.1 Freeze the Authoritative Operational Lifecycle

The authoritative workflow recorded by FD-009 remains:

```text
Mobilize
↓
Review
↓
Validate
↓
Founder Review
↓
Founder Decisions
↓
Validation
↓
Close Review
```

No evidence supports redesigning or adding a new lifecycle state. Register synchronisation remains a mandatory governed operation after Founder Decisions and before post-decision validation:

`Founder Decisions → Synchronise controlled records → Validation`

This operating detail does not amend FD-009.

### 7.2 Reuse Bounded Outputs

Future domains should normally reuse the existing governed structures for:

- Evidence Pack and evidence register entries;
- session or review record;
- Governance Findings and GF register entries;
- consolidated Founder Review Package;
- Founder Decision candidate and validated decision records;
- register and traceability synchronisation;
- Validation Report;
- Closure Report.

An output should not be created merely because D1 had a similarly named file. The Charter, Plan, Execution Rules, and actual risk determine what is required.

### 7.3 Reduce Narrative Duplication

Future domain records should link canonical evidence, findings, decisions, and non-effects rather than reproduce them. A summary should contain only what is needed for the next governed reader or gate.

### 7.4 Apply the Artefact-Necessity Gate

Before creating a new artefact, ask:

> What governance risk does this reduce that is not already controlled elsewhere?

If no additional uncontrolled risk is identified, use or reference an existing controlled record.

### 7.5 Separate Refinement from Redesign

Template improvements, checklist reuse, mechanical validation, and concise cross-references are operational refinements. Framework redesign requires evidence of a genuine governance deficiency and separate authority.
