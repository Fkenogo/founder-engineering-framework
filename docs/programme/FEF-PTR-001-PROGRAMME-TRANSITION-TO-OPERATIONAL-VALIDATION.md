## 1. Purpose

This record documents that the Founder Engineering Framework has completed
its initial engineering programme sufficiently to support controlled
project adoption.

Domains D1 through D6 are formally Closed. The Programme Architecture
Review (FEF-PAR-001) has assessed the resulting Framework, traced sixteen
material claims to repository evidence, and presented four neutral Founder
Options for the programme's next direction. The Founder Engineering
Framework Adoption Guide and its Alignment Template have since been
registered as practical, non-constitutional adoption materials.

Together, these establish a coherent, evidence-traced baseline of the
Framework as it currently stands — sufficient to allow individual Founder
projects to begin controlled, evidence-gathering adoption without
requiring further Framework engineering first. This record exists to make
that programme-level transition explicit, attributable, and bounded. It
does not itself authorise any project's adoption; each project's adoption
requires its own separate authorisation under the Adoption Guide's own
stage process.

## 3. Programme Direction

Future Framework maturity should primarily arise from:

- controlled project adoption, conducted under the Adoption Guide's
  read-only-assessment-first process;
- evidence gathered during real implementation in adopting projects,
  rather than further speculative Framework drafting;
- Founder review of that operational experience as it accumulates; and
- governed Framework Evolution, through the existing FEF-FEV-001 Intake,
  where adoption evidence demonstrates genuine cross-project relevance.

Future engineering work on the Framework itself — including any eventual
D7, D8, or constitutional consolidation — should be driven by demonstrated
need surfaced through this operational evidence, rather than by
speculative governance expansion in the absence of such evidence. This
mirrors PAR-001's own finding (§6) that no governance activity in D1–D6
has yet been operated across more than one Founder project, and treats
that gap as the priority to close before further Framework-level
engineering is undertaken.

## 4. Operational Validation Strategy

Initial adoption should occur through controlled Founder projects, one at
a time or in a small deliberately contrasting pair, rather than a
simultaneous Framework-wide rollout. This follows the Adoption Guide's own
recommended rollout order (§13): select one active, well-documented
project first, review the administrative effort it actually took, and
only then refine and extend the process to further projects.

Each adopting project remains authoritative for its own implementation,
architecture, and delivery decisions. FEF supplies a minimum governance
reference only (Adoption Guide §2–§3); it does not transfer project
authority to the FEF repository, and this record does not change that.

## 5. Expected Outcomes

Adoption is expected to generate the following categories of evidence,
consistent with the Adoption Guide's own Framework Lessons mechanism
(§12):

| Category | What is expected |
|---|---|
| Alignment effectiveness | Whether the FEF baseline materially improves governance clarity in a real project, or adds effort without corresponding value. |
| Unnecessary governance | Controls that a project finds disproportionate to its own risk and scale. |
| Missing controls | Gaps where a project needed a control FEF does not currently provide. |
| Project-specific deviations | Cases where a project intentionally and legitimately differs from the FEF baseline. |
| Reusable patterns | Project-level practices that may generalise usefully to other Founder projects. |
| Framework improvement opportunities | Candidate refinements surfaced by real use, to be routed through FEF-FEV-001 rather than adopted automatically. |

A single project's lesson does not become a Framework rule by itself.
Cross-project adoption of any lesson requires the existing, separately
governed Framework Evolution process.

## 6. Boundaries

This record:

- creates no governance authority;
- creates no constitutional authority;
- creates no implementation authority;
- creates no mandatory project obligation;
- creates no Framework Evolution — FEF-FEV-001 remains the sole
  established mechanism, unaffected by this record; and
- creates no automatic project adoption — each project's adoption
  requires its own separate authorisation under the Adoption Guide's
  Stage 3 Founder review.

It does not amend Framework governance, amend the Constitution, commence
D7, commence D8, commence Framework Evolution, approve any project's
adoption, or approve the Adoption Guide as a governance standard. The
Adoption Guide remains exactly what its own header states: a proposed
operational adoption aid, non-constitutional, non-mandatory beyond
separately authorised project adoption, and subject to revision from real
project use.

## 7. Closing Statement

The programme's focus has transitioned from Framework engineering to
operational validation. D1 through D6 are Closed, PAR-001 is Prepared and
Validated, and the Adoption Guide and Alignment Template are registered as
the practical means by which individual Founder projects may now begin
controlled, evidence-gathering adoption. Further Framework engineering —
including D7, D8, and any constitutional consolidation — remains separate,
uncommenced, future work, to be authorised on its own terms when
operational evidence or Founder decision warrants it.
